Exchanging across zeros

In 3,000 − 847, the top line holds a 0 in every column but the thousands. One thousand can be renamed as 10 hundreds, then one hundred as 10 tens, then one ten as 10 ones.

1,000s 100s 10s 1s 3 0 0 0 − 0 8 4 7 ?

A thousand comes apart without anything being lost:

1000 = 900 + 90 + 10

3000 = 2000 + 900 + 90 + 10

The whole run costs one unit

The thousands drop by one, not by three: the 900, 90 and 10 left in the empty columns came out of that one thousand.

Written down, the 3, 0, 0, 0 becomes 2, 9, 9 and 10. A run of 9s above the top line shows an exchange that had to travel.

3,000 − 847

Ones under ones, tens under tens, hundreds under hundreds.

Across zeros in decimals

In 4.03 − 1.276, writing 4.03 as 4.030 creates a zero of the same kind as the zeros in 3,000: the first exchange is ordinary and the second has to travel.

4.03 − 1.276

Align equal place values

Points beneath points; 4.03 written as 4.030 so the thousandths column has a 0.

In 12 − 3.475, every decimal column of the whole number 12 starts at 0, so the exchange travels from the ones to the thousandths.

12 − 3.475

Align equal place values

Points beneath points; 12 written as 12.000 so the tenths, hundredths and thousandths columns each have a 0.

Change and lengths

£50 − £27.68 is the same subtraction as 50.00 − 27.68: the whole quantity is written to match the decimal, and the unit only says what the answer counts.

Money

A £27.68 purchase is paid for with £50. In 50.00 − 27.68 one ten is exchanged, passes through the ones and tenths, and the change is £22.32.

Measure

A 2.675 m piece is cut from a 5 m length. The 5 is written 5.000, and 5.000 − 2.675 = 2.325 m.

Key points

  • Find the first non-zero digit to the left and reduce it by one.
  • Each empty column passed through receives ten, passes one on, and keeps nine.
  • Subtract using the rewritten digits, including any zeros added after a decimal point.
