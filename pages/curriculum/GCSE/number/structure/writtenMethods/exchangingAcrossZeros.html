Before this page: setting a subtraction out in columns and exchanging one unit from the column on the left, both from column subtraction.

One thousand can reach the ones column

In 3,000 − 847, the top line holds a 0 in every column but the thousands. One thousand can be renamed as 10 hundreds, then one hundred as 10 tens, then one ten as 10 ones.

1,000s 100s 10s 1s 3 0 0 0 − 0 8 4 7 ?

A thousand comes apart without anything being lost:

1000 = 900 + 90 + 10

3000 = 2000 + 900 + 90 + 10

The whole run costs one unit

The thousands drop by one, not by three: the 900, 90 and 10 left in the empty columns came out of that one thousand.

Written down, the 3, 0, 0, 0 becomes 2, 9, 9 and 10. A run of 9s above the top line shows an exchange that had to travel.

Padding a decimal makes zeros that travel the same way

In 4.03 − 1.276, writing 4.03 as 4.030 creates a zero of the same kind as the zeros in 3,000: the first exchange is ordinary and the second has to travel.

4.03 − 1.276

Align equal place values

Points beneath points; 4.03 written as 4.030 so the thousandths column has a 0.

In 12 − 3.475, every decimal column of the whole number 12 starts at 0, so the exchange travels from the ones to the thousandths.

12 − 3.475

Align equal place values

Points beneath points; 12 written as 12.000 so the tenths, hundredths and thousandths columns each have a 0.

Money and measures fill columns with zeros

£50 − £27.68 is the same subtraction as 50.00 − 27.68: the whole quantity is written to match the decimal, and the unit only says what the answer counts.

Money

A £27.68 purchase is paid for with £50. In 50.00 − 27.68 one ten is exchanged, passes through the ones and tenths, and the change is £22.32.

Measure

A 2.675 m piece is cut from a 5 m length. The 5 is written 5.000, and 5.000 − 2.675 = 2.325 m.

Common mistakes

  • Making the column that gave a 9 as well Only the columns the exchange passes through become 9. In 3,000 − 847 the thousands give one away and drop to 2; made a 9 like the rest, the answer reads 9,153 instead of 2,153.
  • Reading the zero that is still written Once a column has been rewritten, the digit above it is the one to use: 9 − 4, not 0 − 4. Reading the printed 0 in the tens of 3,000 − 847 sets up 0 − 4; written as 4, the answer reads 2,143 instead of 2,153.
  • Reversing the subtraction 0 − 7 is not 7. A column that cannot pay is the signal to exchange, and a row of zeros is no exception: answered that way, 3,000 − 847 comes out 3,847.

Summary

  1. A column with nothing to give cannot stop an exchange. The request travels left until it reaches a column that has something.
  2. Every column passed on the way keeps 9 and sends the rest on; the column finally reached drops by one.
  3. 900 + 90 + 10 = 1000, which is why a whole run of 9s costs the column that gave exactly one unit.
  4. Padding a short decimal makes zeros of its own, and they travel in the same way; a whole quantity written to match a decimal, £50 as 50.00, fills its columns with zeros the same way.
  5. Subtract from the rewritten digits, never from the ones they replaced.
