Before this page: know what each digit is worth in integers and decimals, and be able to add two single digits without writing anything down.

One large addition, split by place value

Place value splits 4,687 + 2,759 into additions of thousands, hundreds, tens and ones.

Seven ones and nine ones make sixteen ones whatever stands to the left of them. Sixteen ones is one ten and six ones.

Column addition gives each place its own column. In 342 + 526 no column reaches ten, so every column total is a single digit:

Each column adds quantities of one size only

In 12.4 + 3.75 a digit in the tens column counts groups of ten and a digit in the hundredths column counts groups of one hundredth: each column holds quantities of one size.

Correct alignment
12.40 +03.75

The decimal points line up, so 4 tenths sits over 7 tenths and 0 hundredths over 5 hundredths.

Incorrect alignment
12.4 +3.75

Aligning the final digits puts 5 hundredths beneath 4 tenths and 3 ones beneath 1 ten, so each column of the calculation adds two different sizes.

A zero fills an empty decimal place

Write 12.4 as 12.40, so the hundredths column has a digit in every row; the extra zero changes nothing, 12.40 = 12.4.

Ten in one column is one in the column to its left

A column has room for one digit, so a total of ten or more will not fit; the ten goes to the column on the left, where it is one. Regrouping renames an amount; it never changes it.

Decimals: the columns carry on past the point

In 18.75 + 6.9 the columns carry on past the point: tenths, then hundredths. The point sits between ones and tenths in every row, so the answer's point goes directly beneath the two above:

18.75 + 6.9

Align equal place values

Points beneath points; 6.9 written as 6.90 so the hundredths column has a 0.

The context decides what each column counts

Pounds, metres or minutes: every quantity is converted to the same unit before the columns are set out, and the unit goes with the answer.

Money

A book costs £18.75 and a notebook costs £6.90. Their total cost is £18.75 + £6.90 = £25.65.

Measure

A 2.35 m length is joined to an 85 cm length. 85 cm is 0.85 m, so the total is 2.35 m + 0.85 m = 3.20 m, or 3.2 m.

Three numbers are added in the same columns as two, and a column can now total twenty or more, so 2 is regrouped rather than 1.

Example: add three prices, £18.75 + £6.90 + £4.85

Add the three prices in columns.

  1. Hundredths: 5 + 0 + 5 = 10 Write 0, and regroup 1 into the tenths.
  2. Tenths: 7 + 9 + 8 + 1 = 25 Write 5, and regroup 2 into the ones: twenty-five tenths is two ones and five tenths.
  3. Ones: 8 + 6 + 4 + 2 = 20 Write 0, and regroup 2 into the tens.
  4. Tens: 1 + 2 = 3, so the total is £30.50 The final zero stays, because prices are written to the penny.

Check by subtracting

Subtracting is the inverse of adding, so taking either number from the total gives back the other: 25.65 − 6.90 = 18.75, and the addition was right.

Common mistakes

  • Aligning the final digits 18.75 + 6.9 pushed together at the right-hand edges sets 9 tenths against 5 hundredths and comes to 19.44. Align the decimal points: written as 6.90, every digit stands under a column of its own size, and the total is 25.65.
  • Forgetting a regrouped amount In 4,687 + 2,759 the tens come to 14, and the 1 hundred has to be added into the hundreds rather than only written above them. Left out, the total reads 7,346 instead of 7,446.
  • Writing two digits in one column The ones of 4,687 + 2,759 come to 16, the tens and the hundreds to 13 each, and the three totals written whole give an answer that reads 6131316. The 1 of 16 is one ten, so it joins the tens, 8 + 5 + 1 = 14, and only the 6 stays.

Summary

  1. Align equal place values: points beneath points, and 6.9 written as 6.90 so every column has a digit in every row.
  2. Begin with the furthest occupied column to the right and work left.
  3. Write one result digit in each column and regroup any tens into the column to the left.
  4. Convert quantities to the same unit before adding, and attach that unit to the total.
  5. Check by subtracting either number from the total to recover the other.
