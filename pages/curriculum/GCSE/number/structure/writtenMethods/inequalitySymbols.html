Introduction

Mathematicians use many symbols to describe relationships between numbers. At GCSE level, you will mainly rely on only six of these.

Each remaining symbol describes the relationship between the value on its left and the value on its right.

Equal and not equal

0.6 = 0.60

Equal to

The two decimals are written differently, but both have the same value.

6 ≠ −6

Not equal to

Six and negative six are different values, so the equals sign is crossed out.

It's really that simple for these two — whether the values are the same or different.

Less than and greater than

When two values are different, < and > tell us which is smaller. The point faces the smaller value, while the wide opening faces the larger value.

Drag either point along the line. The comparison can be read in either direction: for example, 4 < 9 means “4 is less than 9”, and also “9 is greater than 4”.

Change the values

Decimals

0.72 < 0.8

Write 0.8 as 0.80. Both whole-number parts are zero, then 7 tenths is less than 8 tenths.

Negative numbers

−3 > −8

−3 is closer to zero, so it lies further right on the number line and is the larger value.

Adding “or equal to”

A short line underneath < or > adds “or equal to”. This means that the value at the limit is now allowed.

The boundary changes

n < 12 leaves out 12, whereas n ≤ 12 includes it.

Likewise, t > − 4 leaves out −4, whereas t ≥ − 4 includes it.

n ≤ 12

n is at most 12

This includes 12 and every value below it.

t ≥ −4

t is at least −4

This includes −4 and every value above it.

Other phrases with the same meanings are:

  • no more than 12 or a maximum of 12 means n ≤ 12

  • no less than 4 or a minimum of 4 means n ≥ 4

Values between two boundaries

Two inequalities can be joined to show that a value lies between two boundaries.

List the integers that satisfy −2 < n ≤ 3

  1. The open left boundary is not included.

  2. The closed right boundary is included.

  3. Read the integer points between the boundaries.

n = −1, 0, 1, 2 or 3

Read from the variable

In −2 < n, the point of the symbol faces −2. Reading from n gives “n is greater than −2”.

Common mistakes

  • Reversing < and >When the values differ, the point faces the smaller value and the wide opening faces the larger value.
  • Forgetting negative order−2 > −7 because −2 lies closer to zero.
  • Missing the equality linex ≤ 5 includes 5, but x < 5 does not.
  • Reading only left to right3 < n means that n is greater than 3.
