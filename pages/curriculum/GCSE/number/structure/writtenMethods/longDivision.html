Dividing by a two-digit number

With a two-digit divisor, keeping the subtractions in your head can be awkward. Long division writes them down. The remainder from each subtraction joins the next digit to form the amount for the next step.

Setting out 1,794 ÷ 23

At each stage, choose the greatest multiple of the divisor that does not exceed the current amount. With the first nine multiples listed, the choice is a matter of looking.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Subtract to find the remainder. It must be smaller than the divisor.

  4. 4

    Bring down the next digit and repeat until every digit of the dividend has been used.

Keeping a zero in the quotient

Once the quotient has begun, every place needs a digit. If the current amount is smaller than the divisor, write a zero in that place before bringing down the next digit. This also applies to the final place of a whole-number quotient.

Remainder or decimal

Once every original digit has been used, stop if the answer is required as a whole-number quotient with a remainder; append a decimal point and zeros only if the answer should continue as a decimal.

Stop with a remainder

1,456 ÷ 32 = 45 remainder 16. After the 6 has been used, 16 remains. It is smaller than 32, so the answer is complete; what a worded question does with the 16 is explained on interpreting a remainder.

Continue as a decimal

1,456 ÷ 32 = 45.5. Write 1,456 as 1,456.0, then bring down the appended zero: 160 ÷ 32 = 5.

Checking by multiplication

Whole-number result

78 × 23 = 1,794, so 1,794 ÷ 23 = 78.

Decimal result

2.85 × 24 = 68.4, so 68.4 ÷ 24 = 2.85.

With a final remainder, multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder: 45 × 32 + 16 = 1,456, the dividend back again.

Equal groups and equal lengths

Sharing 1,794 bolts among 23 crates and cutting 68.4 m into 24 equal lengths have the same structure: the divisor is the number of equal groups or parts, and the quotient is the size of each.

Equal packing

A supplier places 1,794 bolts equally into 23 crates. Each crate contains 78 bolts.

Equal lengths

A 68.4 m cable is cut into 24 equal lengths. Each length is 2.85 m.

Key points

  • Use the largest multiple of the divisor no greater than the current amount.
  • Subtract it, then bring down the next digit. Keep zero quotient digits in their places.
  • For a decimal answer, continue past the point until the remainder is zero or the decimal continues indefinitely.
  • Check by multiplication, adding any remainder back.
