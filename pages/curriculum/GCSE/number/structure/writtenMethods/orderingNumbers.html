  7. Ordering integers, decimals and negatives

Before this page: know what each digit is worth in integers and decimals, since every comparison is made one place value at a time.

Place value decides which number is larger

0.8 is greater than 0.75: 0.8 = 0.80, and 8 tenths is more than 7 tenths, so the hundredths column is never reached.

Compare from the greatest place value towards the smallest. The first place where the digits differ decides the order. On a number line, further right is larger.

Ascending and descending

Ordering numbers means arranging them by size:

  • ascending order — from smallest to largest

  • descending order — from largest to smallest

−3, 0, 2, 4 is in ascending order: it reads left to right along the number line. “Write these numbers in order of size, starting with the smallest” asks for ascending order.

Whole numbers are compared from the greatest place down

Example: put 4,582, 4,528 and 4,805 in ascending order

  1. 4,582 sits between 4,500 and 4,600.
  2. 4,528 is a little further left, so it is smaller than 4,582.
  3. 4,805 is beyond 4,800, making it the largest.

  1. All three numbers have 4 thousands, so compare their hundreds.

  2. 4,805 has 8 hundreds. The other two have 5 hundreds, so 4,805 is the largest.

  3. 4,528 and 4,582 first differ in the tens place. Since 2 tens is less than 8 tens, 4,528 comes first.

Ascending order: 4,528, 4,582, 4,805

More digits means larger, for positive whole numbers only

Any four-digit number is less than any five-digit number: 9,999 < 10,000. For negative numbers more digits does not mean larger: −10 is smaller than −9.

Decimals are compared once their points line up

To compare 3.7, 3.17, 3.705 and 3.07, line up the decimal points so that digits with the same place value sit underneath each other.

Placeholder zeros added to the end of a decimal change nothing, and they give each decimal the same count of places:

3.7 = 3.70 = 3.700

Example: put 3.7, 3.17, 3.705 and 3.07 in ascending order

  1. 3.7 sits seven tenths past 3.
  2. 3.17, or 3.170, is much closer to 3 than 3.7 is.
  3. 3.705 is a little to the right of 3.700.
  4. 3.07, or 3.070, is the closest of the four to 3.
The four decimals in a place value chart

3.7 3 .point 7 0 0
3.17 3 .point 1 7 0
3.705 3 .point 7 0 5
3.07 3 .point 0 7 0

  1. The tenths digits put 3.070 first, 3.170 second, and the two numbers beginning 3.70 last.

  2. 3.700 and 3.705 first differ in the thousandths place, so 3.700 is smaller.

Ascending order: 3.07, 3.17, 3.7, 3.705

A negative number is smaller the further it is from zero

Every negative number is smaller than zero, and so smaller than every positive number.

Among negative numbers, the value closer to zero is larger, so a temperature of −3°C is higher than one of −8°C.

Example: put 5, −1, −6, 0 and 2 in descending order

  1. 5 is the furthest value to the right.
  2. −1 sits one step left of zero, halfway to −2.
  3. −6 is the furthest value to the left.
  4. 0 separates the negative and positive values.
  5. 2 sits between 0 and 5.

  1. The positive numbers come first in descending order: 5, then 2.

  2. Zero comes after the positive numbers and before the negative numbers.

  3. Of the negatives, −1 is closer to zero than −6, so −1 is larger.

Descending order: 5, 2, 0, −1, −6

Split into negatives and positives, then compare each group by place value

In −2.4, 0.6, −2.04, 1 and 0.06, the two negatives are smaller than the three positives, and each group is then put in order by place value.

Example: put −2.4, 0.6, −2.04, 1 and 0.06 in ascending order

  1. −2.4 lies between −2.5 and −2, left of zero.
  2. 0.6 sits between 0.5 and 1.
  3. −2.04 is to the right of −2.4, so it is larger.
  4. 1 is the largest of the five.
  5. 0.06 is positive, a little to the right of zero.

  1. Write the negative decimals as −2.40 and −2.04: 2.40 is further from zero than 2.04, so −2.40 is the smaller.

  2. Write the positive values as 0.06, 0.60 and 1.00. Their place values put them in that order.

Ascending order: −2.4, −2.04, 0.06, 0.6, 1

Common mistakes

  • Treating negatives like positives 9 is larger than 4, but −9 is not larger than −4: it lies further left on the number line, so it is smaller.
  • Counting decimal digits 0.625 is not larger than 0.7: 0.7 is 0.700, and 7 tenths is more than 6 tenths.
  • Adding zeros in the wrong place Zeros go on the end of a decimal: 2.5 = 2.50. Padded to 2.05 instead, it drops below 2.1, and 2.1 and 2.5 come out in the wrong order, as 2.05, 2.1.
  • Answering in the other direction Ascending is smallest first: −6, −1, 0, 2, 5. Writing 5, 2, 0, −1, −6 answers the descending question instead.

Summary

  1. Compare from the greatest place towards the smallest, and the first place where the digits differ decides the order.
  2. Zeros written on the end of a decimal change nothing, so 3.7, 3.17 and 3.705 compare as 3.700, 3.170 and 3.705.
  3. More digits means larger for positive whole numbers only, 9,999 < 10,000; for decimals and negatives it does not, since 0.8 > 0.75 and −10 < −9.
  4. Every negative number is smaller than zero and than every positive number, and among negatives the value closer to zero is the larger, so −2 is greater than −7.
  5. Ascending order runs from smallest to largest and descending order from largest to smallest, which a number line shows as reading left to right or right to left.
