  7. Multiplying and dividing by powers of ten

Before this page: know the place-value columns on either side of the decimal point, and that each is worth ten times the column to its right.

Moving a digit one place changes its value tenfold

In 52 × 10 = 520, the 5 moves from tens to hundreds and the 2 moves from ones to tens. In 5.2 × 10 = 52, the same movement carries the 5 from ones to tens and the 2 from tenths to ones.

The decimal point does not move at all — the digits move past it.

52.8 × 100 = 5,280

The calculation step by step

  1. The multiplier 100 contains two factors of 10, so every digit moves two places to the left.
  2. The 5 moves from the tens place to the thousands place; the 2 moves from the ones place to the hundreds place; and the 8 moves from the tenths place to the tens place.
  3. The ones place is empty, so a zero holds it. The number is 5,280.

Multiplying by 10, 100 or 1,000 moves every digit left, one place for each zero

Multiplying by 100 makes the magnitude a hundred times as large, so 6.47 × 100 is in the hundreds: 647, not 64.7. The two zeros of 100 are its two factors of 10, one place each.

Example 1: calculate 6.47 × 100

  1. 6.47 Start with 6 ones, 4 tenths and 7 hundredths.
  2. Move every digit two places left. Ones become hundreds, tenths become tens and hundredths become ones.
  3. 6.47 × 100 = 647 Six hundreds, four tens and seven ones.

Example 2: calculate 0.39 × 1,000

  1. 3 tenths → 3 hundreds Three places to the left, past the ones.
  2. 9 hundredths → 9 tens The same three places.
  3. 0.39 × 1,000 = 390 A zero holds the empty ones place.

Dividing by 10, 100 or 1,000 moves every digit right, one place for each zero

Dividing a whole number can give a decimal, because the places carry on past the point: the divisor 10 moves every digit of 725 one place to the right, and 725 ÷ 10 = 72.5.

Example 3: calculate 725 ÷ 10

  1. 7 hundreds → 7 tens The new place is worth one tenth of the original place.
  2. 2 tens → 2 ones One tenth of the original place again.
  3. 5 ones → 5 tenths The 5 crosses the point, which stays between ones and tenths.
  4. 725 ÷ 10 = 72.5 Seven tens, two ones and five tenths.

Example 4: calculate 4 ÷ 100

  1. 4 ones → 4 hundredths Two factors of 10 make the place value one hundred times smaller.
  2. Write zeros in the empty places. Both the ones and tenths places must be held by zeros.
  3. 4 ÷ 100 = 0.04 The placeholder zero in the tenths place prevents 4 being read as 4 tenths.

A negative number keeps its sign, and its digits move as before: −3.6 × 100 = −360 and −48 ÷ 1,000 = −0.048.

Multiplying by 0.1 divides by 10, and dividing by 0.1 multiplies by 10

The numbers 0.1 and 0.01 are powers of ten smaller than 1.

Multiplying finds a fraction of the starting value

0.1 is one tenth, so multiplying by 0.1 is equivalent to dividing by 10; multiplying by 0.01 is dividing by 100:

Calculate 6 × 0.1

  1. One tenth of 6 is 6 ÷ 10.
  2. So 6 × 0.1 = 0.6.

Calculate 6 × 0.01

  1. One hundredth of 6 is 6 ÷ 100.
  2. So 6 × 0.01 = 0.06.

Division counts how many fractional pieces fit

The calculation 3 ÷ 0.1 asks how many tenths are in 3:

  1. Each whole contains 10 tenths.
  2. Three wholes contain 3 × 10 = 30 tenths.
  3. So 3 ÷ 0.1 = 30.

Dividing by 0.01 counts hundredths: one whole contains 100 hundredths, so 3 ÷ 0.01 = 300.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the digits the wrong way 3.4 × 10 = 34: multiplying moves every digit one place to the left. Moved to the right instead, the digits give 0.34, a hundred times too small.
  • Always adding zeros 3.4 × 10 = 34, not 3.40: 3.40 is 3.4 again, ten times too small. A zero is written only where a moved digit leaves a place empty.
  • Forgetting placeholder zeros 7 ÷ 100 = 0.07. Writing 0.7 puts the 7 one place too far left, ten times too big.
  • Expecting 8 ÷ 0.1 to be smaller than 8 Each whole holds ten tenths, so 8 ÷ 0.1 = 80, larger than 8; 0.8 is 8 ÷ 10.

Summary

  1. Multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place to the left and dividing by 10 moves every digit one place to the right. The decimal point stays where it is; the digits move past it.
  2. The number of zeros in 10, 100 or 1,000 is the number of places every digit moves.
  3. A zero is written only where the movement leaves a place empty, so 7 ÷ 100 = 0.07 while 3.4 × 10 = 34, not 3.40.
  4. Multiplying by 0.1 is dividing by 10, and multiplying by 0.01 is dividing by 100.
  5. Dividing by 0.1 counts how many tenths fit inside a number, so 8 ÷ 0.1 = 80: dividing a positive number by a value between 0 and 1 makes it larger.
