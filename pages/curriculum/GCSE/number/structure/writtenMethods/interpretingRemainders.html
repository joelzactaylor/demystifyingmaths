  7. Interpreting a remainder

Before this page: be able to divide by a one-digit number and record a remainder using short division.

The words decide the answer

A remainder is the amount left after making as many whole groups as possible. The calculation gives the quotient and remainder; the question says what to do with them.

29 ÷ 4 = 7 remainder 1

29 = 4 × 7 + 1

The 1 is one of the 29, so the answer has to say what happens to it.

A remainder has a fixed range

A remainder must be a whole number from zero up to one less than the divisor. For 29 ÷ 4, 1 < 4. A remainder of 4 would make another complete group.

One division, four possible answers

Example: interpret 29 ÷ 4

29 ÷ 4 = 7 r 1

What is left7 each, 1 left
Exact share7¼ = 7.25 each
Complete groups7 full groups
Groups needed8 groups needed

Start with the division

What the 29 counts decides what the 7 and the 1 mean.

What each wording asks for

“Give the answer with a remainder” or “How many are left?”

What is left

7 remainder 1, or in a context, 1 counter left.

“How much does each receive?”

Exact share

If the quantity can be split, the 1 is shared too: 7¼ or 7.25 each.

“How many complete groups?”

Complete groups

The 1 left over does not fill another group, so only the 7 full ones count.

“How many are needed?”

Groups needed

When every one of the 29 must have a place, the 1 left over needs an 8th group.

This is not ordinary rounding

29 ÷ 4 = 7.25 rounds to 7, yet 29 passengers need 8 cars; choosing between the 7 and the 8 is the subject of rounding in context.

Fractions and decimals describe the same share

When the remainder can be divided, place it over the divisor:

7 r 1→7 + ¼→7¼=7.25

The denominator is 4, not 29: the remainder 1 is being shared between the same four groups. A fraction is exact even when the decimal is not terminating: 29 ÷ 3 = 9⅔, and 9.666… has to be rounded, to the accuracy the question gives.

Three questions, one calculation

A theatre group has 94 costumes. Each rail holds 8 costumes. The calculation is 94 ÷ 8 = 11 remainder 6.

How many rails can be filled?

Only complete rails count, so the answer is 11 full rails.

How many costumes remain after filling them?

11 rails hold 88 costumes, and 94 − 88 = 6 costumes remain.

How many rails are needed for every costume?

The 6 remaining costumes need another rail, so 12 rails are needed.

Each answer includes a unit. “11 remainder 6” records the arithmetic; the nouns turn its two numbers into costumes and rails.

Common mistakes

  • Writing the remainder after the point 47 ÷ 5 = 9 r 2 is not 9.2. The 2 left over is two fifths of a group, so the exact value is 9.4.
  • Always rounding to the nearest whole number 31 people travelling 5 to a car need 7 cars, not 6: 31 ÷ 5 = 6.2 rounds to 6, but 6 cars hold only 30.
  • Giving a fraction of an indivisible object Half a box is not a box: 26 boxes shared between 4 vans is 6 each with 2 left over, not 6.5 each.
  • Dropping the remainder 43 counters shared between 6 pupils is not 7 each with nothing over: 6 × 7 = 42, and the 43rd counter is left over, so the answer is 7 each, 1 left.

Summary

  1. Divide and check that the remainder is smaller than the divisor.
  2. The noun in the question says what the quotient and the remainder count, and the question says which of them it wants.
  3. State the remainder, give an exact share, count complete groups or add another group as the context requires.
  4. A context can require the next whole group or only the complete groups, regardless of ordinary rounding.
  5. The answer carries its unit: 11 rails or 6 costumes, never a bare 11 remainder 6.
