The value a place carries

Before this page: be able to read and write whole numbers and say which digit is which, which the KS2 page on number and place value covers.

Ten symbols, 0 to 9, write every number there is. A digit says how many; the place it stands in says how many of what.

The digit 5 in six places
Number Where the 5 stands What the 5 is worth
5 ones five ones, 5
50 tens five tens, 50
500 hundreds five hundreds, 500
0.5 tenths five tenths, 0.5
0.05 hundredths five hundredths, 0.05
0.005 thousandths five thousandths, 0.005

The symbol is the same in all six. What separates five thousandths from five hundred is the place it was written in.

500 = 5 × 100

  1. The digit 5 says how many.
  2. The hundreds place says how many of what, and 100 is what one hundreds place is worth.

The values of the places

Every place carries a fixed value, running from millions down to thousandths.

The places, from millions to thousandths, with the decimal point between ones and tenths
millions hundred thousands ten thousands thousands hundreds tens ones decimal point. tenths hundredths thousandths
1,000,000 100,000 10,000 1,000 100 10 1 0.1 0.01 0.001

Digits to the left of the point make the whole-number part, and digits to the right make the decimal part. One step left multiplies a place by 10 and one step right divides it by 10, so hundreds, tens and ones are followed by tenths.

Commas group whole-number digits in threes, which is why a million is written 1,000,000 rather than 1000000.

The ones place is the centre

Tens and tenths sit either side of ones, and hundreds and hundredths outside them. The mirror is the ones place rather than the point, which is why the first place to the right of the point is tenths and not oneths.

Zeros that hold a place

Six thousand and forty has no hundreds. A zero stands in the hundreds place to hold it open, and without that zero the remaining digits close up into a different number.

6040

six thousand and forty

The 6 is 6 thousands. The marked zero holds the hundreds place.

640

six hundred and forty

With the hundreds place unheld, the 6 slides into it and the number is smaller by 5,400.

The same holds after the point. Seven thousandths is 0.007, where zeros hold the tenths and hundredths places open; 0.7 is seven tenths, a hundred times as much.

A zero written after the last decimal digit behaves differently: 0.860 and 0.86 are the same number, because the extra zero adds no thousandths. A zero after the last whole-number digit is never spare, since 470 is ten times 47.

Reading a number aloud

Take 4,582.307 apart digit by digit, and each digit names a quantity.

Example: read 4,582.307 aloud

Name each digit by its place, gather the whole-number part, then the decimal part.

  1. 4,000 + 500 + 80 + 2 The whole-number digits are 4 thousands, 5 hundreds, 8 tens and 2 ones.
  2. four thousand five hundred and eighty-two Said in groups of three from the largest, the whole-number part reads as one phrase.
  3. 0.307 is 307 thousandths The last decimal digit stands in the thousandths place, so all three decimal digits count thousandths together.
  4. four thousand five hundred and eighty-two, and three hundred and seven thousandths The whole-number part and the decimal part are said one after the other.

Three tenths, no hundredths and seven thousandths are counted in three different places, so they cannot be added as they stand. A tenth is a hundred thousandths and a hundredth is ten thousandths, which puts all three parts in the same place.

The digits were already saying this. Reading 307 as one number and naming it by the place of its last digit gives 307 thousandths without any rewriting.

Building a number from its words

The reverse builds figures from words. Each group of words names a place, and that place fixes where its digits belong.

six million, three hundred and twelve thousand, forty-seven and eighty-six hundredths

Six million fills the millions group with 6. Three hundred and twelve thousand fills the thousands group with 312. Forty-seven has no hundreds, so a zero holds that place and the group is 047, which makes the whole-number part 6,312,047.

Every group of three carries its own hundreds, tens and ones, and the group's name says what they count. In the thousands group, 312 is 3 hundreds, 1 ten and 2 ones of thousands. The same reading works after the point: eighty-six hundredths fills the thousandths group with 8 hundreds, 6 tens and 0 ones of thousandths, which is 860 thousandths.

860 thousandths is 0.860, and dropping the final zero leaves the same 860 thousandths, so the decimal part is written .86 and the number is 6,312,047.86.

Read any number

The same reading works on any number from 1,000,000 down to 0.001.

    Common mistakes

    • Wrong idea: six thousand and forty is 640 Why it is wrong. The words name no hundreds, so a zero has to hold that place; 640 puts the 6 in the hundreds and is 5,400 short. Correct form. 6,040
    • Wrong idea: the 7 in 4,582.307 is worth 7 Why it is wrong. The 7 stands in the thousandths place, so it counts thousandths rather than ones. Correct form. seven thousandths, 0.007
    • Wrong idea: eighty-six hundredths is 0.086 Why it is wrong. 0.086 puts the 6 in the thousandths place, which makes it eighty-six thousandths, a tenth of the amount named. Correct form. 0.86
    • Wrong idea: a zero on the end never changes a number Why it is wrong. After the last decimal digit a zero adds nothing, but after the last whole-number digit it multiplies by ten. Correct form. 0.860 = 0.86, while 470 = 10 × 47

    Remember

    1. A digit's value is the digit multiplied by its place, so the 5 in 500 is worth 5 × 100.
    2. The places run millions, hundred thousands, ten thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens and ones, then tenths, hundredths and thousandths, each one ten times the place to its right.
    3. The places mirror around the ones place rather than the decimal point, so the first place to the right of the point is tenths.
    4. A zero holds a place open, which is why six thousand and forty is 6,040 and not 640.
    5. A zero after the last decimal digit changes nothing, since 0.860 = 0.86, while a zero after the last whole-number digit multiplies by ten.
