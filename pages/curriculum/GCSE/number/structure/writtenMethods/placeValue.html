500 = 5 × 100

  1. The digit 5 says how many.
  2. The hundreds place says what they are: hundreds, each worth 100.

The 7 of 4,582.307 stands in the thousandths place, so it is worth 7 × 0.001 = 0.007; “write down the value of the 7 in 4,582.307” asks for 0.007, or 7 thousandths. The 6 in 6,312,047.86 is three places to the left of the 6 in 6,040, so it is worth a thousand times as much: 6 million rather than 6 thousand.

Digits to the left of the point make the whole-number part, and digits to the right make the decimal part. Each step to the left multiplies the place by 10 and each step to the right divides it by 10.

The commas mark off the whole-number digits in threes from the right: 6,312,047 is 6 million, 312 thousand and 47.

The ones place is the centre

The place names pair outward from ones: tens with tenths, and hundreds with hundredths. The decimal point separates ones from tenths, so the first place to its right is tenths and there is no oneths place.

Zeros that hold a place

Six thousand and forty has no hundreds, so a zero holds the hundreds place open:

6040

six thousand and forty

The 6 is 6 thousands.

640

six hundred and forty

Without that zero the 6 drops into the hundreds place, and the number is 5,400 smaller.

After the point too: seven thousandths is 0.007, with zeros holding the tenths and hundredths places open, while 0.7 is seven tenths, a hundred times as much.

A zero written after the last decimal digit behaves differently: 0.860 and 0.86 are the same number, because the extra zero adds no thousandths. A zero after the last whole-number digit is not spare: 470 is ten times 47.

The decimal part is read by its last place

4,582.307 is 4,000 + 500 + 80 + 2 + 0.3 + 0.007; the 0 in the hundredths place adds nothing:

Example: read 4,582.307 aloud

Say the whole-number part, then the decimal part.

  1. four thousand five hundred and eighty-two The 4 is the thousands group and 582 the ones group: four thousand, then five hundred and eighty-two.
  2. 0.307 is 307 thousandths The last decimal digit stands in the thousandths place, so all three decimal digits count thousandths together.
  3. four thousand five hundred and eighty-two, and three hundred and seven thousandths An “and” joins the two parts.

Read digit by digit, the decimal part is “point three zero seven”; the place reading, three hundred and seven thousandths, is the one that says what the digits are worth. Three tenths, no hundredths and seven thousandths are counted in three different places, so each is written in thousandths first:

Words become figures group by group

six million, three hundred and twelve thousand and forty-seven, and eighty-six hundredths

Every group of three has its own hundreds, tens and ones, and the group's name says what quantity they count: in the thousands group, 312 is 3 hundreds, 1 ten and 2 ones of thousands. After the point the name gives the last digit's place: eighty-six hundredths ends in the hundredths place, so the 6 goes there and the 8 in the tenths, .86. The number is 6,312,047.86, and “write six million, three hundred and twelve thousand and forty-seven in figures” asks for 6,312,047.

    Common mistakes

    • Leaving out a zero that holds a place Three thousand and five names no hundreds and no tens, so two zeros hold those places: 3,005. Writing 305 puts the 3 in the hundreds and is 2,700 short.
    • Reading a digit by its face value The 3 in 60.03 stands in the hundredths place, so it counts three hundredths, 0.03, rather than three ones.
    • Counting hundredths as thousandths Forty-seven hundredths written as 0.047 puts the 7 in the thousandths place, so it is forty-seven thousandths, a tenth of the amount. Forty-seven hundredths is 0.47.

    Summary

    1. A digit's value is the digit multiplied by its place, so the 5 in 500 is worth 5 × 100.
    2. The places run millions, hundred thousands, ten thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens and ones, then tenths, hundredths and thousandths, each one ten times the place to its right.
    3. Place names pair outward from ones — tens with tenths, hundreds with hundredths — so the first place to the right of the decimal point is tenths.
    4. A zero holds a place open, which is why six thousand and forty is 6,040 and not 640.
    5. A zero after the last decimal digit changes nothing, since 0.860 = 0.86, while a zero after the last whole-number digit multiplies by ten.
    6. The decimal part is read as one number of its last place: 0.307 is three hundred and seven thousandths.
    7. Whole-number digits group in threes from the right, and each group has its own hundreds, tens and ones: 6,312,047 is 6 million, 312 thousand and 47.
