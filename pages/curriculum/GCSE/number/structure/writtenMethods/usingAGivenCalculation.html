One known calculation generates a family of facts

Before this page: know how to multiply and divide by powers of ten, and know basic multiplication and division facts.

43 × 26 = 1,118 fixes a relationship between two factors and their product. Scaling one factor produces a predictable scale change in the product.

For 4.3 × 26, compare each value with the known calculation:

  1. What changed?43 → 4.3 The first factor was divided by 10.
  2. What stayed the same?26 → 26 The second factor did not change.
  3. Make the product follow1,118 → 111.8 Divide the product by 10 as well.

Therefore 4.3 × 26 = 111.8. One scale relationship has extended the known product to a new multiplication fact.

Scale one factor at a time

Write each change beside the number it affects. This makes the product's change visible before any decimal point is placed.

Given: 43 × 26 = 1,118

43 × 26 = 1,118, so 4.3 × 26 = 111.8 and 4.3 × 2.6 = 11.18.

Combine the scale changes

Each factor of 10 changes the product once. In 4.3 × 2.6, the first factor is one tenth of 43 and the second is one tenth of 26.

product change=first-factor change × second-factor change

Two divisions by 10 combine to make a division by 100

The product does not merely follow the number of decimal places that happen to be visible. It follows the size change in each factor: one tenth × one tenth = one hundredth.

Read a product fact backwards

Every multiplication fact gives two division facts. From 43 × 26 = 1,118, it follows immediately that:

Division undoes multiplication. Once the related product 4.3 × 2.6 = 11.18 is known, make 11.18 the dividend and divide by either factor. The quotient is the other factor.

Form the related multiplication fact first, then read it in the direction the question requires.

Same product, smaller known factor

4.3 × 260 = 1,118

4.3 is one tenth of 43. To keep the product at 1,118, the other factor must be ten times 26.

1,118 ÷ 4.3 = 260

Product and divisor both smaller

43 × 2.6 = 111.8

The second factor and product are both one tenth of their original values, so the other factor remains 43.

111.8 ÷ 2.6 = 43

The first factor is divided by 10 and the second factor is unchanged, so the product is divided by 10. The related fact is 4.3 times 26 equals 111.8.

Keep the scale relationships connected

  • Changing the product only once If both factors are divided by 10, the product is divided by 10 twice: overall, it is divided by 100.
  • Moving every decimal point the same way Compare the role of each number first. In 1,118 ÷ 4.3, a smaller divisor produces the larger quotient 260.
  • Recalculating the given product Treat 43 × 26 = 1,118 as known. Only apply the stated powers-of-ten changes.
  • Forgetting the inverse fact Rewrite a missing factor as division or a missing dividend as multiplication before tracking the changes.

Summary

  1. Treat the stated calculation as a fact that connects two factors and their product.
  2. Write how each factor has changed: ×10, ÷10, ×100, ÷100 or unchanged.
  3. Combine the two factor changes to find the product's scale change.
  4. Use inverse operations to turn the related fact into the form needed for a quotient or missing value.
  5. Check with the inverse operation and confirm that the new values have the expected scale.
