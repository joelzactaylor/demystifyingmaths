Moving along the line

Before this page: be able to place negative numbers and decimals in order from ordering positive and negative numbers, and read the value of a digit from place value in integers and decimals.

Every number can be drawn as an arrow on the number line. A positive number points right and a negative number points left. The arrow's length is the magnitude of the number, so 6 runs six units to the right and −4 runs four units to the left. The first arrow has its tail on zero.

Example: two moves from −4

−4 + 6 − 5 = −3

The first arrow

−4 is an arrow four units long, drawn from zero and pointing left.

−4 + 6 − 5 = −3

How the arrows are laid

Adding lays the next arrow tip to tail: its tail sits on the tip of the one before, and the total moves along to its tip.

Subtracting lays it tip to tip: its tip meets the tip of the one before, and the total moves back to its tail.

Counting one unit at a time works for short arrows and becomes slow for long ones. The length of the arrow is what the arithmetic gives: −27 + 40 = 13, because 27 of the 40 units reach zero and 13 remain.

Example: work out −7 + 3

Start at −7 and add 3.

  1. Start at −7. Seven units to the left of zero.
  2. Adding 3 moves 3 right. The 3 is positive, so the move runs towards the larger numbers.
  3. −7 + 3 = −4 Three units from −7 reach −4, which is still left of zero because 3 is smaller than 7.

A decimal moves by the same rules. −2.5 + 1.8 = −0.7, because an arrow 1.8 long is not enough to reach zero from −2.5 and leaves 0.7 of the distance still to go.

Two signs meeting

Subtracting a number is adding it with its sign changed. Changing the sign of 5 gives −5, and changing the sign of −3 gives 3.

Subtracting −3 lays an arrow pointing left with its tip on the current total, so its tail lands three units to the right. That is the same move adding 3 makes.

3 − (−2) = 3 + 2 = 5

5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8

  • Subtracting a negative number moves right, so the total increases.
  • Adding a negative number moves left, so the total decreases.

The first sign is the operation and the second belongs to the number. Combining them changes the operation and leaves the number untouched: −4 − (−9) = −4 + 9 = 5 still has its 9, and 3 + (−7) = 3 − 7 = −4 still has its 7.

Example: work out −4 − (−9)

Subtract −9 from −4.

  1. The signs between the numbers are − and −. A subtraction sign followed by a negative number.
  2. −4 − (−9) = −4 + 9 Subtracting −9 adds 9, the same number with its sign changed.
  3. −4 + 9 = 5 Four of the nine units reach zero, and five remain.

The gap between two numbers

The difference between two numbers is the distance between them along the line, counted as a positive amount. Zero falls between −6°C and 4°C, so the rise from one to the other is made of two parts.

Example: the rise from −6°C to 4°C

−6°C to 4°C

The colder reading

−6°C stands six degrees below zero.

4 − (−6) = 10

Subtracting the lower reading from the higher one gives that distance without counting: 4 − (−6) = 4 + 6 = 10, a rise of 10 degrees. Subtracting the other way round gives −10, a fall of the same 10 degrees.

Example: a balance moving from −£85.40 to £32.10

Work out how much was paid in.

  1. The balance rises, so the amount paid in is the increase. −£85.40 is an overdraft of £85.40.
  2. 32.10 − (−85.40) = 32.10 + 85.40 Subtracting a negative adds, so the two amounts combine.
  3. 32.10 + 85.40 = 117.50 £85.40 clears the overdraft and £32.10 is left, so £117.50 was paid in.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong idea: −6 + 4 = −10 Why it is wrong. This adds 6 and 4 and keeps the minus. Adding 4 moves 4 to the right, towards zero, so the answer is closer to zero than −6 is. Correct form. −6 + 4 = −2
  • Wrong idea: 5 − (−3) = 2 Why it is wrong. This reads the two signs as a single minus and takes 3 away. The second sign belongs to the 3, so the number being subtracted is −3, and subtracting it adds 3. Correct form. 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8
  • Wrong idea: the difference between −6°C and 4°C is 2 degrees Why it is wrong. This subtracts 4 from 6 and ignores the side of zero each reading sits on. The gap runs 6 degrees up to zero and 4 degrees beyond it. Correct form. 4 − (−6) = 10 degrees

Remember

  1. An arrow points right for a positive number and left for a negative one, and its length is the number's distance from zero.
  2. −7 + 3 = −4: adding less than the distance to zero leaves the answer negative.
  3. Subtracting a number is adding it with its sign changed, so 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8; adding a negative runs left, so 3 + (−7) = 3 − 7 = −4.
  4. The difference between two numbers is the distance between them, and subtracting the lower from the higher gives it: 4 − (−6) = 10.
  5. Decimals move by the same rules, so −2.5 + 1.8 = −0.7.
