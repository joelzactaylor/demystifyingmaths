Before this page: be able to read a base and its index from index notation and powers, know the sign of a product from multiplying and dividing negative numbers, and know that indices are worked first from the order of operations.

Is −3² equal to −9 or to 9?

BIDMAS works indices before adding and subtracting, and that settles this one too: the squaring is done first, and the minus is applied to whatever it produces.

−3^2 and (−3)^2

Two ways to read three symbols

The index is written against the 3 on the left and against the bracket on the right.

−3^2 = −9, and (−3)^2 = 9

A bracket is the only thing that puts the minus inside the squaring. Nothing else changes: the same order of operations gives 5 − 3^2 = 5 − 9 = −4.

Example: work out −5² and (−5)²

Work out each of the two readings.

  1. 5^2 = 25 The index stands against the 5 in both.
  2. −5^2 = −25 No bracket, so the minus stays outside the squaring.
  3. (−5)^2 = 25 The bracket puts the minus inside, and two negatives make a positive.

The index says how many negatives are multiplied

Once a bracket has put the minus inside, the index counts the factors, and every one of them is negative. So the index alone decides the sign, without any of them being written out.

Two negatives multiplied together make a positive, so the factors pair off into positive products. An odd index leaves one with nothing to pair with, and that one turns the answer negative.

(−2)^5

Five negative factors

The index counts how many copies of −2 are multiplied together.

(−2)^5 = −32

The size never depends on the sign: 2^5 = 32 gives the 32 in (−2)^5 as well.

A power of a negative number

Work out the power of the number without its sign. Then make the answer negative if the index is odd, and leave it positive if the index is even.

Example: work out (−2)⁶

Take the size and the sign separately.

  1. 2^6 = 64 The power of the number without its sign.
  2. 6 is even Six negatives make three pairs, with none left over.
  3. (−2)^6 = 64 An even index leaves the answer positive.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong idea: −3^2 = 9 Why it is wrong. This squares the minus along with the 3, which is what a bracket would have asked for. The index stands against the 3 alone, so the squaring gives 9 and the minus is still in front of it. Correct form. −3^2 = −9, while (−3)^2 = 9
  • Wrong idea: (−2)^4 = −16 Why it is wrong. This keeps a minus because the number being multiplied was negative. Four negatives make two pairs and each pair is positive, so nothing is left to carry a minus. Correct form. (−2)^4 = 16
  • Wrong idea: (−2)^3 = 8 Why it is wrong. This takes every power of a negative number to be positive, which holds for squares because two negatives pair off. Three negatives leave one unpaired. Correct form. (−2)^3 = −8

Remember

  1. BIDMAS works the index before the minus, so −3^2 = −9.
  2. A bracket gathers the minus in with the number, so (−3)^2 = 9.
  3. The index counts the negative factors: an even index leaves the answer positive, an odd one leaves it negative.
  4. The size is the same power of the number without its sign: 2^5 = 32.
