Before this page: be able to read a base and its index from index notation and powers, and work out a root of a perfect square from square roots.

Does 3 + 4 × 5 come to 23 or to 35?

Adding first leaves 7 × 5, which is 35; multiplying first leaves 3 + 20, which is 23. Neither working has a slip in it; the answers differ only in which was done first.

By agreement, multiplying and dividing are done before adding and subtracting, so 3 + 4 × 5 = 3 + 20 = 23.

Three operations allow more orders, and 2 + 3 × 4 − 1 comes to four different answers depending on which is worked first.

The agreed order, the × first, gives 13. A cake at £5 and three coffees at £4 cost 5 + 3 × 4 = 17 pounds. Gathering 5 + 3 first would charge £32.

Example: work out 10 − 2 × 3

Take 2 × 3 from 10.

  1. The terms are 10 and 2 × 3. The minus sign separates them.
  2. 2 × 3 = 6 Two threes make one amount of 6.
  3. 10 − 2 × 3 = 4 The 6 stands where 2 × 3 stood: 10 − 6 = 4.

Brackets make their contents one amount

Brackets are how to ask for the 3 + 4 to be done first:

3 + 4 × 5 and (3 + 4) × 5

A rectangle 4 by 5

4 × 5 is a rectangle: four squares up the side, five along the bottom, 20 in all.

A pair of brackets gathers its contents into one amount before anything outside is worked. In 2 × 3 + 4 × 5, the bracket placement decides which numbers meet first.

2 × 3 + 4 × 5 = 26

Brackets round either product in 2 × 3 + 4 × 5 change nothing, because each product is gathered before the adding anyway.

Example: insert brackets in 3 + 5 × 2 to make 16

Find the placing that gives 16.

  1. 3 + 5 × 2 = 13 Five twos make one amount of 10, and 3 + 10 = 13.
  2. (3 + 5) × 2 = 16 The 2 now multiplies 3 + 5 rather than the 5 alone: eight taken twice.

Powers and roots before the multiplying around them

A power is worked before the multiplying around it: 3 × 4^2 is three sixteens, 48. A square root is worked at the same stage: 5 × √9 = 15.

2 + 3 × 4^2 ÷ 8

The power first

4 × 4 = 16 is worked before the × and the ÷ either side of it.

2 + 3 × 4^2 ÷ 8 = 8

  • Brackets first, and the innermost pair before the pair around it.
  • Then powers and roots.
  • Then multiplying and dividing, from the left.
  • Then adding and subtracting, from the left.

BIDMAS spells the same order in six letters: brackets, indices, division, multiplication, addition, subtraction. The D and M are one strength and so are the A and S: D before M does not mean divide before multiply, and A before S does not mean add before subtract.

Example: work out 40 ÷ 5 × 2

Divide 40 by 5, then multiply by 2.

  1. 40 ÷ 5 = 8 Dividing and multiplying are one strength, so the leftmost of the two comes first.
  2. 40 ÷ 5 × 2 = 16 Eight doubled. Multiplying first would give 40 ÷ 10 = 4, a different calculation.

Bars that group without brackets

A root's bar fixes how far the root reaches. √(9 + 16) has both numbers under one bar, so the 9 + 16 is added before the root is taken, and the answer is 5. Shortening the bar to cover the 9 alone leaves the 16 outside it, and √9 + 16 = 19.

A fraction bar groups the same way, one amount above it and one below, so (12 + 8)/(5 − 1) is 20 ÷ 4, which is 5.

Example: work out (7 + 5)/(9 − 3)

Divide 7 + 5 by 9 − 3.

  1. 7 + 5 = 12 The bar groups 7 + 5 as one amount.
  2. 9 − 3 = 6 The bar groups 9 − 3 the same way.
  3. 12 ÷ 6 = 2 The bar divides one amount by the other.

Common mistakes

  • Working straight from the left 2 + 6 × 3 is not 24. The 6 × 3 is worked before the addition, so the amount added to 2 is 18: 2 + 6 × 3 = 2 + 18 = 20.
  • Multiplying before the index is applied In 2 × 5^2 the index sits on the 5 alone, so the 5 is squared and the 2 multiplies the 25: 2 × 25 = 50, not 100.
  • Rooting a sum term by term One bar covers all of 36 + 64, so the number under it is 100 and √(36 + 64) = 10, not 14.
  • Adding before subtracting 15 − 6 + 4 is not 5. Adding and subtracting have the same strength, so the 15 − 6 on the left is worked first: 9 + 4 = 13.

Summary

  1. By convention multiplying and dividing come before adding and subtracting: 3 + 4 × 5 = 3 + 20 = 23.
  2. Brackets are how to ask for a different grouping, so (3 + 4) × 5 = 35.
  3. Powers and roots are worked before the multiplying around them: 3 × 4^2 = 48.
  4. Operations of the same strength are worked from the left: 40 ÷ 5 × 2 = 16.
  5. A root's bar and a fraction bar group what is under them, so √(9 + 16) = 5.

Optional: implied multiplication can be ambiguous

Two readings, so the form needs a bar or a bracket

Extra info

A number written against a bracket, as in 8(1 + 3), is a multiplication with its sign left out. After a ÷ sign, as in 4 ÷ 8(1 + 3), it is read two ways, and no universal convention settles which.

Reading ÷ and × from left to right gives 4 ÷ 8 × 4 = 2; doing the implied multiplication first gives 4 ÷ 32 = 0.125. Both readings occur in calculators and texts.

A division bar or a bracket round the product leaves one reading only: 4/(8(1 + 3)) = 0.125, or (4 ÷ 8)(1 + 3) = 2.

Style guides and ISO 80000-2, the international standard for mathematical notation, ask for that bracket or bar rather than settling which reading is right. Dave Peterson works through both conventions and reaches the same conclusion in Implied Multiplication 1: Not as Bad as You Think (archived).
