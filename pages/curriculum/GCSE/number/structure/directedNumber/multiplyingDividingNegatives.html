  7. Multiplying and dividing negative numbers

Several arrows of the same length

Before this page: be able to lay negative numbers out as arrows on the line from adding and subtracting negative numbers, and divide by a one-digit number using short division.

Multiplying by a positive whole number lays the same arrow down again and again. Three arrows of −4, laid tip to tail from zero, finish at −12. Together they reach as far as one arrow three times as long.

Example: three arrows of −4

(−4) × 3

One arrow of −4

−4 runs four units to the left of zero.

(−4) × 3 = −12

Swapping the two numbers leaves the product unchanged, so 3 × (−4) = −12 as well. One negative number multiplied by a positive one gives a negative product, whichever way round the two are written.

Example: work out 6 × (−7)

Multiply 6 by −7.

  1. 6 × 7 = 42 The two distances from zero multiply to give the length of the answer.
  2. One of the numbers is negative. Six arrows of −7 all run to the left.
  3. 6 × (−7) = −42 An arrow 42 units long, pointing left.

Stepping the second number past zero

Arrows can be laid out three times or twice, never −2 times. (−3) × (−2) is reached instead by the steps between the products that come before it.

Lowering the second number by one takes away one copy of the first: (−3) × 3 = −9 and (−3) × 2 = −6, a move of 3 to the right. The same move of 3 happens at every step down, and it carries on as the second number passes zero.

Example: the second number stepping down to −2

(−3) × 3 down to (−3) × (−2)

Three lots of −3

The arrow reaches −9, nine units to the left of zero.

(−3) × (−2) = 6

Multiplying by a negative number turns the arrow round, and the size of that number fixes the length it becomes. Turning −3 round gives 3, which is what (−3) × (−1) = 3 says. Stretching that arrow to twice its length gives (−3) × (−2) = 6.

(−3) × (−2) = 6

  • One negative number turns the arrow round once, so the product is negative.
  • Two negative numbers turn it round twice, so the product is positive.
  • A zero gives an arrow of no length, so the product is zero and has no sign.

A decimal product takes its sign the same way. 0.8 × (−5) = −4 has one negative number in it, and (−0.6) × (−7) = 4.2 has two. The digits there multiply as 6 × 7 = 42, and the point is placed by the method on multiplying decimals.

Example: work out (−5) × (−6)

Multiply −5 by −6.

  1. 5 × 6 = 30 The two distances from zero multiply to give the length of the answer.
  2. Both numbers are negative. The arrow for −5 is turned round twice, which leaves it pointing right.
  3. (−5) × (−6) = 30 A right-pointing arrow 30 units long.

Dividing, by cutting and by counting

Dividing cuts an arrow into equal parts, or counts how many of one arrow fit inside another. The arrow for −12 can be cut into three equal parts, and arrows of −3 can be laid tip to tail from zero until they reach it.

Example: cutting −12 and filling it

−12 ÷ 3 and −12 ÷ (−3)

Twelve units to the left

−12 is one arrow running from zero to twelve below it.

−12 ÷ 3 = −4 and −12 ÷ (−3) = 4

A quotient takes its sign from the same count as a product. −12 ÷ 3 = −4 has one negative number and is negative; −12 ÷ (−3) = 4 has two and is positive.

Arrows of −3 laid from zero never reach 12, however many of them there are. Division has multiplication as its inverse, so 12 ÷ (−3) is the number that multiplies −3 to give 12. That number is −4, because (−3) × (−4) = 12.

Example: work out −4.2 ÷ 6

Divide −4.2 into six equal parts.

  1. 4.2 ÷ 6 = 0.7 Sharing 42 tenths between 6 gives 7 tenths.
  2. One of the numbers is negative. Six equal parts of a left-pointing arrow all point left.
  3. −4.2 ÷ 6 = −0.7 Each part is an arrow 0.7 long, pointing left.

A decimal divisor is turned into a whole number first, by the method on dividing by a decimal. The sign is then settled by the same count: −7.2 ÷ 0.9 = −8.

Longer products

Each negative number in a product turns the arrow round once. Two turns leave it facing the way it started, so (−2) × 3 × (−5) = 30. A third turns it away again, so (−1) × (−2) × (−3) = −6.

Counting the negatives

The size of the answer comes from multiplying the numbers without their signs: 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.

An even count of negative numbers leaves the answer positive, and an odd count leaves it negative.

(−2) × 3 × (−4) = 24

Example: work out (−1) × (−2) × (−3)

Multiply the three numbers together.

  1. 1 × 2 × 3 = 6 The three distances from zero multiply to give the length of the answer.
  2. Three of the numbers are negative. Three turns leave the arrow pointing left, as one turn does.
  3. (−1) × (−2) × (−3) = −6 An arrow 6 units long, pointing left.

A negative divisor turns the arrow round in the same way, so ((−6) × (−4)) ÷ (−3) = 24 ÷ (−3) = −8 has three negative numbers and a negative answer.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong idea: −12 ÷ (−3) = −4 Why it is wrong. This divides 12 by 3 and writes a minus in front because both numbers had one. Four arrows of −3 reach −12, and the answer counts those arrows, so it is 4. Correct form. −12 ÷ (−3) = 4
  • Wrong idea: −4 − 3 = 7 Why it is wrong. This turns two negatives into a positive in a subtraction. Nothing is being multiplied: starting at −4 and moving a further 3 to the left reaches −7. Correct form. −4 − 3 = −7
  • Wrong idea: (−1) × (−2) × (−3) = 6 Why it is wrong. This pairs off two of the negatives and drops the third. The third turns the arrow round once more, and it finishes pointing left. Correct form. (−1) × (−2) × (−3) = −6

Remember

  1. Three arrows of −4 laid tip to tail reach −12, so (−4) × 3 = −12.
  2. Multiplying by a negative number turns the arrow round, so (−3) × (−2) = 6.
  3. One negative number makes a product or a quotient negative, and two make it positive: 0.8 × (−5) = −4.
  4. A division answers a multiplication, so 12 ÷ (−3) = −4, since (−3) × (−4) = 12.
  5. In a longer product, an even count of negative numbers leaves the answer positive: (−2) × 3 × (−5) = 30.
