Reading a power backwards

Before this page: be able to read and evaluate powers using index notation.

Evaluating 3^4 gives 81. Recognising a power reverses that calculation: starting with 81, supply an equal power.

evaluate3^4 ⟶ 81

recognise81 ⟶ 3^4

The final numeral does not reveal its equal factors. Recognition depends on knowing the power facts that produce it.

Squares and cubes

A square has two equal factors.

  1. 1^21
  2. 2^24
  3. 3^29
  4. 4^216
  5. 5^225
  6. 6^236
  7. 7^249
  8. 8^264
  9. 9^281
  10. 10^2100
  11. 11^2121
  12. 12^2144
  13. 13^2169
  14. 14^2196
  15. 15^2225

A cube has three equal factors. The cubes to know here are:

  1. 1^31
  2. 2^38
  3. 3^327
  4. 4^364
  5. 5^3125
  6. 10^31,000

Both 0 and 1 are square and cube numbers: 0^2 = 0^3 = 0, and 1^2 = 1^3 = 1.

Known powers give both matches and bounds. An exact match recognises a power; a value strictly between consecutive powers is ruled out.

50 is not a squareIt lies between consecutive squares.7^2 = 49 < 50 < 64 = 8^2

100 is not a cubeIt lies between consecutive cubes.4^3 = 64 < 100 < 125 = 5^3

Powers of 2, 3, 4 and 5

Each row begins with its base and repeatedly multiplies by that same base.

Powers of 2
2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024
Powers of 3
3, 9, 27, 81, 243
Powers of 4
4, 16, 64, 256, 1,024
Powers of 5
5, 25, 125, 625, 3,125

243appears fifth in the powers of 3243 = 3^5

200lies between consecutive powers of 2128 < 200 < 256

One number can have several power names

The six factors of 2 in 64 can be grouped into three factors of 4 or two factors of 8.

64 = 2^6 = 4^3 = 8^2

Changing the base changes the index. Swapping them is not valid: 4^3 = 64, while 3^4 = 81.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong idea: 4^3 = 81 Why it is wrong. 81 is 3^4, read with the base and the index the wrong way round. Three factors of 4 come to 64. Correct form. 4^3 = 64, and 3^4 = 81
  • Wrong idea: 64 cannot be written as a power of 4 Why it is wrong. The six factors of 2 in 64 group into three factors of 4, and into two factors of 8. Correct form. 64 = 2^6 = 4^3 = 8^2

Remember

  1. Recognising a power reverses evaluating one: 81 is 3^4 because four factors of 3 multiply to 81.
  2. The squares worth recalling run 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196 and 225.
  3. The cubes worth recalling are 1, 8, 27, 64 and 125, together with 10^3 = 1,000.
  4. A value between two consecutive powers is not itself a power, so 49 < 50 < 64 rules 50 out as a square number.
  5. One number can carry several power names, since 64 = 2^6 = 4^3 = 8^2, but swapping a base and its index changes the value.
