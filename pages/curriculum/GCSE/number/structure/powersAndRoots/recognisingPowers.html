The direction with no method

Before this page: be able to read and work out a power, from index notation and powers.

3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81. Four multiplications, each one a fact you already have.

Now start from 81. Nothing about it points at 3. There is a method for going one way and none for coming back.

So you learn the numbers instead. Of the first 256, twenty-six are a square, a cube, or a power of 2, 3 or 5.

The squares, and the gaps between them

The longest list is the squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, and on to 15^2 = 225.

Fifteen numbers, but not fifteen separate facts. Each square is the one before it with a row and a column added, and that addition is the next odd number.

The cubes, and how fast they climb

The cubes are fewer, and they climb faster: 5^2 = 25 and 5^3 = 125.

A cube of side n is n layers of n by n.

The cube is turned by dragging it, or with the arrow keys.

Cubes up to 5³

One cube

A cube of side 1 is a single unit cube, so 1³ = 1.

1, 8, 27, 64, 125

  • 1^3 = 1
  • 2^3 = 8
  • 3^3 = 27
  • 4^3 = 64
  • 5^3 = 125
  • 10^3 = 1,000

Note — 10^3 is a thousand: the step from millimetres to metres, and from grams to kilograms.

Where the lists overlap

Three lists look like three things to learn. 64 is on all three, and the same 64 counters make each one.

64 counters, arranged three ways

64 counters

Sixty-four counters

Scattered, they are just 64 things.

64 = 8^2 = 4^3 = 2^6

That happens whenever one base is a power of another. 4 is 2^2 and 8 is 2^3, so every power of 4 and every power of 8 is already a power of 2.

Doubling on from 32: 2^6 = 64, 2^7 = 128, 2^8 = 256, 2^9 = 512, 2^10 = 1,024.

Note — 2^6 is 64 while 6^2 is 36. The base and the index are not interchangeable.

When the number is on none of them

81 is on the lists. 4,096 is not, and neither is 200.

Three questions settle it without recognising anything: is it one of the squares, is it one of the cubes, and does halving it reach 1? A power of 2 halves all the way down: 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1.

For 144

A whole number from 2 to 10,000.

144

Three questions settle it: is it a square, is it a cube, and does halving take it to 1?

Halving 4,096 twelve times reaches 1, so it is 2^12. Halving 200 reaches 25, which is odd, and 200 is on none of the three lists. Dividing is slower than knowing, and it always answers.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong idea — 6^2 = 12 Why it is wrong. Squaring multiplies a number by itself; doubling is a different operation. Correct form. 6^2 = 6 × 6 = 36
  • Wrong idea — 64 is a power of 2, so it is not a power of 4 Why it is wrong. One number can be a power of several bases at once. Correct form. 64 = 8^2 = 4^3 = 2^6
  • Wrong idea — 200 is even, so it is a power of 2 Why it is wrong. Halving 200 reaches 25, which will not halve again. Correct form. The powers of 2 either side of 200 are 2^7 = 128 and 2^8 = 256.
  • Wrong idea — 2^6 = 6^2 Why it is wrong. The base and the index do different jobs, so swapping them gives a different number. Correct form. 2^6 = 64 while 6^2 = 36

Remember

  1. A method turns 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 into 81; only the lists turn 81 back into 3^4.
  2. The squares run to 15^2 = 225, and each is the one before it plus the next odd number.
  3. The cubes are 1, 8, 27, 64 and 125, and 10^3 = 1,000.
  4. The lists overlap: 64 is 8^2, 4^3 and 2^6, because 4 and 8 are themselves powers of 2.
  5. For a number on none of the lists, check the squares, check the cubes, then halve it: dividing is slower than knowing and always answers.
