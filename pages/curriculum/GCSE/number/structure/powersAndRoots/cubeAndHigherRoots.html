The cube root

Before this page: be able to cube a number using index notation, and recall the cubes of 1 to 5 and 10 from recognising powers of a number.

3 × 3 × 3 = 27. The cube root of 27 is the number that was cubed: 3.

27 unit cubes pack into a cube with 3 along every edge, so 3 is the edge length that goes with a volume of 27. A cube root is that edge.

³√27 = 3

  1. 27 stands under the sign, and it is the volume of the cube.
  2. 3 is the value of the root, and it is that cube's edge.

The small 3 in the crook of the sign is the order, and it says how many equal factors the root is looking for. A square root shows no order, because 2 is the order left unwritten.

Example: work out ³√125

Match the number to a cube, read the number that was cubed, then cube it back.

  1. ³√125 125 stands under the sign, so the question is which number three equal factors multiply to.
  2. 5 × 5 × 5 = 125 125 is the cube of 5, one of the cubes worth recalling.
  3. ³√125 = 5 Cubing 5 returns the 125 that stood under the sign, which is the check.

The cube root of a negative number

(−3) × (−3) × (−3) = −27, so ³√(−27) = −3.

The first two negative factors multiply to a positive 9, and the third turns that back to −27. An odd count of negative factors leaves the product negative.

A square has two equal factors carrying the same sign, so nothing on the number line squares to −9. A cube has three, and three negative factors reach −27.

One root, with nothing for ± to separate

Two numbers square to 49, which is why √49 names one of them and x^2 = 49 is answered by x = ±7. One number on the line cubes to −27, so ³√(−27) is −3 and there is no second value to record.

Higher roots

An order of 4 or 5 works the same way. ⁵√32 = 2, because 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.

ⁿ√x

  1. n is the order, written in the crook of the sign.
  2. The root is the number that n equal factors multiply to make x.

Example: work out ⁴√16

Read the order, then find the number that many equal factors multiply to.

  1. ⁴√16 The order is 4, so the question asks for four equal factors.
  2. 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16 Four factors of 2 come to 16, working one factor at a time: 2, 4, 8, 16.
  3. ⁴√16 = 2 Raising 2 to the fourth power returns the 16 under the sign.

The root keys

A calculator carries a cube root key, and a key marked with a raised x before the sign for any other order. Key the order, then that key, then the number: 5 and 32 give 2.

Odd and even roots

An odd order reaches every number on the line: ³√(−27) = −3 and ⁵√(−32) = −2.

An even count of negative factors leaves a positive product, so an even order reaches 0 and the positive numbers only. A square root is that case with the order left unwritten.

ⁿ√x

  1. n odd: one number on the line, whatever the sign of x.
  2. n even and x above 0: two numbers on the line, and the sign names the non-negative one.
  3. n even and x below 0: no number on the line.

Common mistakes

  • Wrong idea: ³√(−27) = 3 Why it is wrong. 3 × 3 × 3 = 27, which is positive, so 3 cubes to 27 rather than to −27. Correct form. ³√(−27) = −3
  • Wrong idea: ³√(−27) = ±3 Why it is wrong. ± separates two values, and 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 shows that 3 is not one of them. Correct form. ³√(−27) = −3
  • Wrong idea: ³√27 = 9 Why it is wrong. 9 is 27 divided by 3, and dividing is not rooting: 9 × 9 × 9 = 729. Correct form. 3^3 = 27, so ³√27 = 3
  • Wrong idea: ⁴√(−16) = −2 Why it is wrong. The order 4 is even, and (−2)^4 = 16, which is positive. Correct form. ⁴√(−16) has no value on the number line

Remember

  1. A cube root is the edge of a cube with that volume: ³√27 = 3, because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
  2. The order in the crook counts the equal factors the root is looking for, and a square root leaves the order 2 unwritten.
  3. An odd order reaches every number on the line and reaches each one once, so ³√(−27) = −3 and there is no second value for ± to record.
  4. An even order reaches no negative number, so ⁴√(−16) has no value on the number line.
