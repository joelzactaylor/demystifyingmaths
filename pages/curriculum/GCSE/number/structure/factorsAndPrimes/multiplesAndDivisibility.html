Before this page: be able to list the factors of a number in pairs, and to divide and see what is left over from short division.

Counting in steps

Counting in 6s gives 6, 12, 18, 24. It runs on for as long as anyone keeps counting, and those are the multiples of 6.

Turn 24 round and 6 is one of its factors. The two words name one relationship from either end; the factors stop at 24, and the multiples never stop.

Where two counts meet

Count in 4s and in 6s along the same line. Some places are reached by both counts.

The common multiples of 4 and 6 up to 36

Two counts along one line

Counting in 4s and counting in 6s land together at some places and nowhere else.

12, 24 and 36

12 is where the two counts first meet. That makes it the lowest common multiple of 4 and 6, and the later meetings are its multiples.

Splitting a number in two

Does 4 divide 4,932? Dividing it out answers that, but it is a long calculation for a yes or a no.

A shorter route starts from one way of writing any number:

Every number is (a multiple of 10) + (its last digit)

  1. 4,932 = 4,930 + 2
  2. 70 = 70 + 0
  3. 8 = 0 + 8

The first part of that split is worth something. 4,930 ends in a 0, so it is a whole number of tens, and a ten is two 5s and five 2s.

4,930 divides by 2, by 5 and by 10 exactly. Whatever the answer turns out to be for 4,932, that part of it cannot be the reason.

Only the 2 is left to decide, and 2 is even. That is the whole of the test for 2: split off the last digit and look at it.

When one digit is not enough

Try the same split for 4, and it breaks down. 4,930 is a whole number of tens, but a ten is not a whole number of 4s.

Cutting one place further left fixes it. A hundred is 25 fours, so everything above the last two digits is settled before the cut is made.

Does 4 divide 4,932?

One number, cut in two

The big part looks after itself, and whatever is left over is what decides.

4,932 = 4,900 + 32, and 32 = 4 × 8

That gives a second rule, shaped like the first. Every number is (a multiple of 100) + (its last two digits), and the 4 test reads the second half of it.

9 is the awkward one

No cut anywhere in 4,932 helps. Ten is not a whole number of 9s, a hundred is not, and neither is a thousand.

Each of them misses by exactly 1, though. Ten is 9 + 1, a hundred is 99 + 1, and a thousand is 999 + 1, and that turns out to be enough.

So take 4,932 apart place by place. Each place then splits the same way.

Does 9 divide 4,932?

4,932, taken apart by place

Every place splits into a part made of 9s and a single digit left over.

4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18, so 4,932 = 9 × 548

That is why adding the digits works. Each digit is exactly what its own place leaves behind, so the sum of them is what the whole number leaves behind.

999, 99 and 9 are multiples of 3 as well. The same sum settles both questions, and 18 puts 4,932 in both lists.

The tests, and what makes each one safe

  1. 2 the last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 every ten is five 2s
  2. 5 the last digit is 0 or 5 every ten is two 5s
  3. 10 the last digit is 0 every ten is one 10
  4. 4 the last two digits make a multiple of 4 every hundred is twenty-five 4s
  5. 3 the digits add to a multiple of 3 9, 99 and 999 are all multiples of 3
  6. 9 the digits add to a multiple of 9 9, 99 and 999 are all multiples of 9

A digit sum can be added again. 18 gives 1 + 8 = 9, and each round leaves a smaller number in the same list.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping factor and multiple 6 is a factor of 24, not a multiple of it. The multiples of 6 are the numbers counting in 6s reaches: 6, 12, 18, 24, and none of them is smaller than 6.
  • Adding the digits for a test that does not use them The digits of 4,527 add to 18, which is a multiple of 9, and 4,527 is odd. Adding the digits tests 3 and 9; the 2 test reads the last digit alone.
  • Testing 4 on the last digit alone 518 ends in 8, and 8 is a multiple of 4, but 518 ÷ 4 = 129.5. A ten is not a whole number of 4s, so the tens digit still counts: 18 is no good, while 516 works because 16 is.
  • Taking any common multiple for the lowest one 24 is reached by counting in 4s and by counting in 6s, but the two counts have already met at 12. The lowest common multiple is the first meeting, not any of them.

Remember

  1. The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and onwards, and 6 is a factor of every one of them.
  2. A common multiple of 4 and 6 is reached by both counts, and 12 is the first.
  3. Every test cuts the number into a part that is certainly a multiple of the divisor and a part left over, and only the leftover can decide.
  4. A ten is a whole number of 2s, 5s and 10s, so those tests cut after the last digit; a hundred is a whole number of 4s, so that one cuts after the last two.
  5. No power of ten is a whole number of 9s, but each is 1 more than one, so every place leaves its own digit behind and the digits are added instead.
