Before this page: be able to list the factors of a number in pairs, and to divide and see what is left over from short division.

Counting in steps

Counting in 6s gives 6, 12, 18, 24. It runs on for as long as anyone keeps counting, and those are the multiples of 6.

Turn 24 round and 6 is one of its factors. The two words name one relationship from either end; the factors stop at 24, and the multiples never stop.

Where two counts meet

Count in 4s and in 6s along the same line. Some places are reached by both counts.

The common multiples of 4 and 6 up to 36

Two counts along one line

Counting in 4s and counting in 6s land together at some places and nowhere else.

12, 24 and 36

12 is where the two counts first meet. That makes it the lowest common multiple of 4 and 6, and the later meetings are its multiples.

Why a test can work at all

Dividing 4,518 by 4 answers the question the slow way. A test reads it off the digits instead.

Ten is five 2s, two 5s and one 10. So every column above the ones is settled already, and only the last digit can leave anything over.

4 is different. Ten is not a whole number of 4s, but a hundred is twenty-five of them, so the test reads the last two digits.

9 is the awkward one

No power of ten is a whole number of 9s. Each is one more than one that is: 10 is 9 + 1, 100 is 99 + 1, and 1,000 is 999 + 1.

Does 9 divide 4,518?

4,518, taken apart by place

Every place splits into a part made of 9s and a single digit left over.

4 + 5 + 1 + 8 = 18, so 4,518 = 9 × 502

999, 99 and 9 are multiples of 3 as well. The same sum settles both questions, and 18 puts 4,518 in both lists.

The tests, and what makes each one safe

  1. 2 the last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 every ten is five 2s
  2. 5 the last digit is 0 or 5 every ten is two 5s
  3. 10 the last digit is 0 every ten is one 10
  4. 4 the last two digits make a multiple of 4 every hundred is twenty-five 4s
  5. 3 the digits add to a multiple of 3 9, 99 and 999 are all multiples of 3
  6. 9 the digits add to a multiple of 9 9, 99 and 999 are all multiples of 9

A digit sum can be added again. 18 gives 1 + 8 = 9, and each round leaves a smaller number in the same list.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping factor and multiple 6 is a factor of 24, not a multiple of it. The multiples of 6 are the numbers counting in 6s reaches: 6, 12, 18, 24, and none of them is smaller than 6.
  • Adding the digits for a test that does not use them The digits of 4,527 add to 18, which is a multiple of 9, and 4,527 is odd. Adding the digits tests 3 and 9; the 2 test reads the last digit alone.
  • Testing 4 on the last digit alone 518 ends in 8, and 8 is a multiple of 4, but 518 ÷ 4 = 129.5. Both of the last two digits count: 18 is no good, while 516 works because 16 is.
  • Taking any common multiple for the lowest one 24 is reached by counting in 4s and by counting in 6s, but the two counts have already met at 12. The lowest common multiple is the first meeting, not any of them.

Remember

  1. The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and onwards, and 6 is a factor of every one of them.
  2. A common multiple of 4 and 6 is reached by both counts, and 12 is the first.
  3. The 2, 5 and 10 tests read the last digit, because every ten is a whole number of each; the 4 test reads the last two.
  4. 9, 99 and 999 are multiples of both 3 and 9, which is why adding the digits answers both: 4 + 5 + 1 + 8 = 18.
