  7. Multiples and divisibility tests

Before this page: be able to list the factors of a number in pairs, and to divide and see what is left over from short division.

A multiple of 6 is 6 times a whole number

Counting on in 6s gives 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, and never ends. Those are the multiples of 6, each 6 times a whole number, so 6 is a factor of every one of them.

A division decides whether a number is on the list: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 with nothing over, so 24 is a multiple of 6; 25 ÷ 6 = 4 r 1, so 25 is not, and the same goes for any number that leaves a remainder.

The last digit tests 2, 5 and 10

Short division decides whether 4 divides 4,932, but it takes four steps of working for a yes or a no.

A divisibility test reads the digits and gives the yes or the no without the division; the quotient, when it is wanted, still has to come from dividing.

Divisible by 2

The last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8.

The last digit of 4,932 is 2. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 2 = 2,466

Why it works

Every whole number is a multiple of 10 with its last digit added on: 4,932 = 4,930 + 2. A ten is five 2s, so 4,930 is a whole number of 2s and leaves nothing over; only the 2 on the end is left to decide. 2 is one whole 2, so nothing is left over at all.

Change the number to 4,933 and the 4,930 is untouched; 3 is one 2 with 1 left over: 4,933 ÷ 2 = 2,466 r 1. That is the whole test: 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 are whole numbers of 2s, and the other five digits each leave 1 behind.

Divisible by 5

The last digit is 0 or 5.

The last digit of 4,932 is 2. No: 4,932 ÷ 5 = 986 r 2

Why it works

4,932 = 4,930 + 2, and a ten is two 5s, so 4,930 is a whole number of 5s and leaves nothing over. Only the 2 on the end is left to decide, and 2 is smaller than 5, so the remainder is 2.

Divisible by 10

The last digit is 0.

The last digit of 4,932 is 2. No: 4,932 ÷ 10 = 493 r 2

Why it works

4,932 = 4,930 + 2, and 4,930 is 493 tens, so it divides by 10 exactly. Only the 2 on the end is left to decide, and every digit from 1 to 9 is smaller than 10, so each one survives as the remainder. Only a 0 leaves nothing behind.

The last two digits test divisibility by 4

Divisible by 4

The number made by the last two digits is a multiple of 4.

The last two digits of 4,932 make 32, which is 4 × 8. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 4 = 1,233

Why it works

A hundred is a whole number of 4s, 100 = 4 × 25, so 4,932 is cut after its last two digits: 4,932 = 4,900 + 32. The 4,900 is 49 hundreds and leaves nothing over; only the 32 is left to decide.

One digit is not enough, because a ten is not a whole number of 4s: 10 = 4 × 2 + 2.

The digit sum tests 3 and 9

Divisible by 9

The digits add to a multiple of 9.

Adding the digits of 4,932 gives 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18, and 9 divides 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 9 = 548

Why it works

No cut helps for 9, because a ten, a hundred and a thousand each miss a multiple of 9 by 1: 10 = 9 + 1; 100 = 99 + 1; 1,000 = 999 + 1.

So each place of 4,932 splits into a whole number of 9s with its own digit left over: 4,000 = 4 × 999 + 4; 900 = 9 × 99 + 9; 30 = 3 × 9 + 3; and the 2 is a single digit already.

The parts made of 999s, 99s and 9s add to 4 × 999 + 9 × 99 + 3 × 9 = 4,914 = 9 × 546, a multiple of 9 whatever the digits are. The digits left over add to 18 = 9 × 2, two more 9s.

Adding the digits of the digit sum keeps the remainder: 18 is a ten and an 8, the ten is 9 + 1, so 18 and 1 + 8 = 9 leave the same remainder.

Divisible by 3

The digits add to a multiple of 3.

Adding the digits of 4,932 gives 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18, and 3 divides 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 3 = 1,644

Why it works

9, 99 and 999 are multiples of 3 as well, 3 × 3, 3 × 33 and 3 × 333, so the split for 9 works for 3 too. The part of 4,932 made of 999s, 99s and 9s is 4,914 = 3 × 1,638, and the digits left behind add to 18 = 3 × 6.

The two tests read the same sum and can answer differently. 4,524 adds to 15, which 3 divides and 9 does not, so 4,524 ÷ 3 = 1,508 while 4,524 ÷ 9 = 502 r 6.

Six tests on one number

Every whole number answers all six tests from its digits alone; the division only confirms what the digits said.

A whole number with three or four digits.
  1. 2 The last digit is 5. No: 2,835 ÷ 2 = 1,417 r 1
  2. 5 The last digit is 5. Yes: 2,835 ÷ 5 = 567
  3. 10 The last digit is 5. No: 2,835 ÷ 10 = 283 r 5
  4. 4 The last two digits make 35. No: 2,835 ÷ 4 = 708 r 3
  5. 9 The digits add to 2 + 8 + 3 + 5 = 18. Yes: 2,835 ÷ 9 = 315
  6. 3 The digits add to 2 + 8 + 3 + 5 = 18. Yes: 2,835 ÷ 3 = 945

2,835 is a multiple of 3, 5 and 9, and not of 2, 4 or 10.

A whole number with three or four digits is needed.

Common mistakes

  • Calling 6 a multiple of 24 6 is a factor of 24, not a multiple of it: the multiples of 24 are 24, 48, 72 and so on, and 6 is not among them.
  • Testing 2 on the digit sum The digits of 4,527 add to 18, which is even, and 4,527 is odd: 4,527 ÷ 2 = 2,263 r 1. Adding the digits tests 3 and 9; the test for 2 reads the last digit alone.
  • Testing 4 on the last digit alone 734 ends in 4, and 4 is a multiple of 4, but 734 ÷ 4 = 183 r 2: 34 is not a multiple of 4, while 732 passes because 32 is.
  • Looking for a 9 instead of adding the digits 4,536 contains no digit 9, but its digits add to 18 and 4,536 ÷ 9 = 504. The digit sum, not the presence of a 9, decides the test.

Summary

  1. The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and onwards, and 6 is a factor of every one of them.
  2. The last digit tests divisibility by 2, 5 and 10; the last two digits test divisibility by 4.
  3. The digit sum tests divisibility by 3 and 9.
  4. A divisibility test gives a yes or a no; division is still needed to find the quotient.

Optional extension

Beyond this GCSE lesson

The six GCSE tests use 2, 3, 4, 5, 9 and 10. Tests for 6, 7, 8, 11, 12 and 25 extend the same place-value reasoning.

Powers of ten also give tests for 8 and 25

Divisible by 25

The last two digits are 00, 25, 50 or 75.

The last two digits of 4,932 make 32. No: 4,932 ÷ 25 = 197 r 7

Why it works

A hundred is four 25s, so the cut comes after the last two digits, as it does for 4: 4,932 = 4,900 + 32. The 4,900 is 49 × 4 = 196 twenty-fives and leaves nothing over; the 32 holds one 25 with 7 left over.

Of the pairs of digits from 00 to 99, only 00, 25, 50 and 75 are whole numbers of 25s.

Divisible by 8

The number made by the last three digits is a multiple of 8.

The last three digits of 4,932 make 932, and 932 ÷ 8 = 116 r 4. No: 4,932 ÷ 8 = 616 r 4

Why it works

A thousand is a whole number of 8s, 1,000 = 8 × 125, so the cut comes after the last three digits: 4,932 = 4,000 + 932. The 4,000 is 4 × 125 = 500 eights and leaves nothing over; only the 932 is left to decide.

Two digits are not enough, because a hundred is not a whole number of 8s: 100 = 8 × 12 + 4. 132 ends in 32, which is 4 × 8, but 132 ÷ 8 = 16 r 4.

Coprime tests combine for 6 and 12

Divisible by 6

The number passes the test for 2 and the test for 3.

4,932 ends in 2, and its digits add to 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 6 = 822

Why it works

6 = 2 × 3, and 2 and 3 have no common factor above 1. So a number that is a multiple of 2 and a multiple of 3 is a multiple of 6.

4,932 is even, so 4,932 = 2 × 2,466. Its digits add to 18, so 3 divides it as well; 3 is not a factor of 2, so it is a factor of 2,466, and 2,466 = 3 × 822. Together, 4,932 = 2 × 3 × 822 = 6 × 822.

Divisible by 12

The number passes the test for 3 and the test for 4.

The digits of 4,932 add to 18, and its last two digits make 32. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 12 = 411

Why it works

12 = 3 × 4, and 3 and 4 have no common factor above 1. So a number that is a multiple of 3 and a multiple of 4 is a multiple of 12.

Alternating digits test divisibility by 11

Divisible by 11

The digits, added and subtracted alternately from the right, make a multiple of 11.

For 4,932: 2 − 3 + 9 − 4 = 4, and 11 does not divide 4. No: 4,932 ÷ 11 = 448 r 4

Why it works

Every place value lands within 1 of a multiple of 11, alternately under and over: a ten is 1 under 11, a hundred is 1 over 99 = 11 × 9, and a thousand is 1 under 1,001 = 11 × 91. That alternation is where the plus and minus in the test come from.

Split 4,932 that way: 4 × 1,001 − 4 for the thousands, 9 × 99 + 9 for the hundreds, 3 × 11 − 3 for the tens, and the 2.

The multiples of 11 add to 4,004 + 891 + 33 = 4,928 = 11 × 448. What remains is 2 − 3 + 9 − 4 = 4; since 4 is smaller than 11, it is the remainder.

Starting from the other end flips every sign, giving 4 − 9 + 3 − 2 = −4. Since 11 divides −4 exactly when it divides 4, either direction answers the test.

Doubling the last digit tests divisibility by 7

Divisible by 7

Remove the last digit, double it, and subtract the double from the number that is left. Repeat until the answer is small enough to recognise.

From 4,932, 493 − 4 = 489; from 489, 48 − 18 = 30, and 7 does not divide 30. No: 4,932 ÷ 7 = 704 r 4

Why it works

7 divides no power of ten: 10 ÷ 7 = 1 r 3, 100 ÷ 7 = 14 r 2 and 1,000 ÷ 7 = 142 r 6. So the last digits cannot be read on their own; the test shrinks the whole number a step at a time instead.

The first step on 4,932 gives 493 − 2 × 2 = 489. Then 489 × 10 = 4,890, which is 42 below 4,932, and 42 = 7 × 6. The two numbers are a multiple of 7 apart, so either both divide by 7 or neither does. Since 7 is not a factor of 10, 4,890 = 10 × 489 divides by 7 exactly when 489 does.

The gap is a multiple of 7 because the last digit was doubled: with a last digit b and a in front of it, 10 × (a − 2b) sits 21b below 10a + b, and 21 = 7 × 3.
