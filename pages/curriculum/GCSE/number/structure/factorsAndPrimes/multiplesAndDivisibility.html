Before this page: be able to list the factors of a number in pairs, and to divide and see what is left over from short division.

Counting in steps

Counting on in 6s gives 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, and never ends. Those are the multiples of 6, each 6 times a whole number, so 6 is a factor of every one of them. A division decides whether a number is on the list: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 with nothing over, so 24 is a multiple of 6; 25 ÷ 6 = 4 r 1, so 25 is not, and the same goes for any number that leaves a remainder.

Testing a number without dividing it

Does 3 divide 4,932? Dividing it out answers that, but it is a long calculation for a yes or a no. A divisibility test reads the digits and gives the yes or the no without the division; the quotient, when it is wanted, still has to come from dividing. GCSE asks for six tests: four that read the last digits, and two that add them.

Reading the last digits

Divisible by 2

The last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8.

The last digit of 4,932 is 2. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 2 = 2,466

Why it works

Every counting number is a multiple of 10 with its last digit added on: 4,932 = 4,930 + 2. A ten is five 2s, so 4,930 is a whole number of 2s and leaves nothing over; only the 2 on the end is left to decide. 2 is one whole 2, so nothing is left over at all: 4,932 ÷ 2 = 2,466.

Change the number to 4,933 and the 4,930 is untouched. Only the last digit has moved, and 3 is one 2 with 1 left over: 4,933 ÷ 2 = 2,466 r 1. That is the whole test: 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 are whole numbers of 2s, and the other five digits each leave 1 behind.

Divisible by 5

The last digit is 0 or 5.

The last digit of 4,932 is 2. No: 4,932 ÷ 5 = 986 r 2

Why it works

4,932 = 4,930 + 2, and a ten is two 5s, so 4,930 is a whole number of 5s and leaves nothing over. Only the 2 on the end is left to decide, and 2 is smaller than 5, so the whole 2 stays behind as the remainder: 4,932 ÷ 5 = 986 r 2.

Of the ten digits, only 0 and 5 are whole numbers of 5s. 1, 2, 3 and 4 are too small to make a 5 at all, and 6, 7, 8 and 9 make one 5 with some over. That is why every multiple of 5 ends in 0 or 5.

Divisible by 10

The last digit is 0.

The last digit of 4,932 is 2. No: 4,932 ÷ 10 = 493 r 2

Why it works

4,932 = 4,930 + 2, and 4,930 is 493 tens, so it divides by 10 exactly. Only the 2 on the end is left to decide, and every digit from 1 to 9 is smaller than 10, so each one survives as the remainder: 4,932 ÷ 10 = 493 r 2. Only a 0 leaves nothing behind.

Divisible by 4

The number made by the last two digits is a multiple of 4.

The last two digits of 4,932 make 32, which is 4 × 8. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 4 = 1,233

Why it works

A hundred is a whole number of 4s, 100 = 4 × 25, so the cut comes after the last two digits: 4,932 = 4,900 + 32. The 4,900 is 49 hundreds and leaves nothing over; only the 32 is left to decide, and 32 = 4 × 8, so 4,932 ÷ 4 = 1,233.

One digit is not enough, because a ten is not a whole number of 4s: 10 = 4 × 2 + 2. 518 ends in 8, which is 4 × 2, but its last two digits make 18, which is 4 × 4 + 2, and that 2 is the remainder: 518 ÷ 4 = 129 r 2.

Adding the digits

Divisible by 9

The digits add to a multiple of 9.

Adding the digits of 4,932 gives 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18, and 9 divides 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 9 = 548

Why it works

No cut helps for 9: a ten is not a whole number of 9s, a hundred is not, and neither is a thousand. Each misses by exactly 1: 10 = 9 + 1; 100 = 99 + 1; 1,000 = 999 + 1.

So each place of 4,932 splits into a whole number of 9s and a single digit left over: 4,000 = 4 × 999 + 4; 900 = 9 × 99 + 9; 30 = 3 × 9 + 3; and the 2 is a single digit already. Every place hands back its own digit.

The parts made of 999s, 99s and 9s add to 4 × 999 + 9 × 99 + 3 × 9 = 4,914 = 9 × 546, which needs no testing. The digits handed back add to 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18 = 9 × 2, two more 9s, so 4,932 ÷ 9 = 548.

A digit sum too big to recognise can be added again: 18 is a ten and an 8, the ten is 9 + 1, so 18 and 1 + 8 = 9 leave the same remainder.

Divisible by 3

The digits add to a multiple of 3.

Adding the digits of 4,932 gives 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18, and 3 divides 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 3 = 1,644

Why it works

9, 99 and 999 are multiples of 3 as well: three 3s, thirty-three 3s and 333 of them, so the split for 9 works for 3 too. The part of 4,932 made of 999s, 99s and 9s is 4,914 = 3 × 1,638, and the digits left behind add to 18 = 3 × 6, so 4,932 ÷ 3 = 1,644.

The two tests read the same sum and can answer differently. 4,524 adds to 15, which 3 divides and 9 does not, so 4,524 ÷ 3 = 1,508 while 4,524 ÷ 9 = 502 r 6.

Six tests on one number

Any three- or four-digit number answers all six from its digits alone, and the divisions only confirm what the digits said.

A whole number with three or four digits.
  1. 2 The last digit is 2. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 2 = 2,466
  2. 5 The last digit is 2. No: 4,932 ÷ 5 = 986 r 2
  3. 10 The last digit is 2. No: 4,932 ÷ 10 = 493 r 2
  4. 4 The last two digits make 32. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 4 = 1,233
  5. 9 The digits add to 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 9 = 548
  6. 3 The digits add to 4 + 9 + 3 + 2 = 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 3 = 1,644

4,932 is a multiple of 2, 3, 4 and 9, and not of 5 or 10.

A whole number with three or four digits is needed.

Beyond the syllabus

Divisible by 25

The last two digits are 00, 25, 50 or 75.

The last two digits of 4,932 make 32. No: 4,932 ÷ 25 = 197 r 7

Why it works

A hundred is four 25s, so the cut comes after the last two digits, as it does for 4: 4,932 = 4,900 + 32. The 4,900 is 49 × 4 = 196 twenty-fives and leaves nothing over; the 32 holds one 25 with 7 left over, so 4,932 ÷ 25 = 197 r 7.

Of the pairs of digits from 00 to 99, only 00, 25, 50 and 75 are whole numbers of 25s, which is the whole test.

Divisible by 8

The number made by the last three digits is a multiple of 8.

The last three digits of 4,932 make 932, and 932 ÷ 8 = 116 r 4. No: 4,932 ÷ 8 = 616 r 4

Why it works

A thousand is a whole number of 8s, 1,000 = 8 × 125, so the cut comes after the last three digits: 4,932 = 4,000 + 932. The 4,000 is 4 × 125 = 500 eights and leaves nothing over; only the 932 is left to decide, and 932 ÷ 8 = 116 r 4, so 4,932 ÷ 8 = 616 r 4.

Two digits are not enough, because a hundred is not a whole number of 8s: 100 = 8 × 12 + 4. 132 ends in 32, which is 4 × 8, but 132 ÷ 8 = 16 r 4.

Divisible by 6

The number passes the test for 2 and the test for 3.

4,932 ends in 2, and its digits add to 18. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 6 = 822

Why it works

6 = 2 × 3, and 2 and 3 have no common factor above 1. So a number that is a multiple of 2 and a multiple of 3 is a multiple of 6.

4,932 is even, so 4,932 = 2 × 2,466. Its digits add to 18, so 3 divides it as well; the 3 cannot be inside the 2, so it is inside the 2,466, and 2,466 = 3 × 822. Together, 4,932 = 2 × 3 × 822 = 6 × 822.

Divisible by 12

The number passes the test for 3 and the test for 4.

The digits of 4,932 add to 18, and its last two digits make 32. Yes: 4,932 ÷ 12 = 411

Why it works

12 = 3 × 4, and 3 and 4 have no common factor above 1. So a number that is a multiple of 3 and a multiple of 4 is a multiple of 12, and 4,932 passes both: its digits add to 18 = 3 × 6, and its last two digits make 32 = 4 × 8, so 4,932 ÷ 12 = 411.

2 and 6 will not do instead, because both of them contain a 2. 18 passes the tests for 2 and for 6, and 18 ÷ 12 = 1 r 6.

Divisible by 11

Add and subtract the digits alternately, starting from the right. If the answer is a multiple of 11, so is the number.

For 4,932: 2 − 3 + 9 − 4 = 4, and 11 does not divide 4. No: 4,932 ÷ 11 = 448 r 4

Why it works

Every place value lands within 1 of a multiple of 11, alternately under and over: a ten is 1 under 11, a hundred is 1 over 99 = 11 × 9, and a thousand is 1 under 1,001 = 11 × 91. That alternation is where the plus and minus in the test come from.

Split 4,932 that way: 4 × 1,001 − 4 for the thousands, 9 × 99 + 9 for the hundreds, 3 × 11 − 3 for the tens, and the 2.

The multiples of 11 add to 4,004 + 891 + 33 = 4,928 = 11 × 448, and what is left is 2 − 3 + 9 − 4 = 4. 4 is smaller than 11, so it is the remainder itself: 4,932 ÷ 11 = 448 r 4.

Starting from the other end flips every sign, giving 4 − 9 + 3 − 2 = −4, and 11 divides −4 exactly when it divides 4. Either direction answers the test, but only the count from the ones digit hands back the remainder as well.

Divisible by 7

Remove the last digit, double it, and subtract the double from the number that is left. Repeat until the answer is small enough to recognise.

From 4,932, 493 − 4 = 489; from 489, 48 − 18 = 30, and 7 does not divide 30. No: 4,932 ÷ 7 = 704 r 4

Why it works

7 divides no power of ten: 10 ÷ 7 = 1 r 3, 100 ÷ 7 = 14 r 2 and 1,000 ÷ 7 = 142 r 6. So no tail of digits can be read on its own, and the test works on the whole number instead, shrinking it a step at a time.

The first step on 4,932 gives 493 − 2 × 2 = 489. Multiplied back up by 10 that is 4,890, which sits 42 below 4,932, and 42 = 7 × 6. Two numbers a multiple of 7 apart leave the same remainder when divided by 7, so 7 divides 4,890 exactly when it divides 4,932; and 4,890 = 10 × 489, where the 10 contributes no 7, so everything now rests on the 489.

The doubling is what lands that gap on a multiple of 7. With a last digit b and a in front of it, 10 × (a − 2b) sits 21b below 10a + b, and 21 = 7 × 3. The step on 489 gives 48 − 2 × 9 = 30, which 7 does not divide, so 4,932 ÷ 7 = 704 r 4.

Common mistakes

  • Calling 6 a multiple of 24 6 is a factor of 24, not a multiple of it. The multiples of 6 are the numbers counting in 6s reaches, 6, 12, 18, 24 and onwards, and none of them is smaller than 6.
  • Testing 2 on the digit sum The digits of 4,527 add to 18, which is even, and 4,527 is odd: 4,527 ÷ 2 = 2,263 r 1. Adding the digits tests 3 and 9; the test for 2 reads the last digit alone.
  • Testing 4 on the last digit alone 518 ends in 8, and 8 is a multiple of 4, but 518 ÷ 4 = 129 r 2. A ten is not a whole number of 4s, so the tens digit still counts: 18 is not a multiple of 4, while 516 passes because 16 is.
  • Testing 12 with the tests for 2 and 6 18 passes both, and 18 ÷ 12 = 1 r 6. Two tests can only be put together when their numbers have no common factor above 1: 3 and 4 give 12, and 2 and 3 give 6, but 2 and 6 both contain a 2.

Remember

  1. The multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24 and onwards, and 6 is a factor of every one of them.
  2. A test can read the last digits alone when some power of ten is a multiple of the divisor: one digit for 2, 5 and 10, two for 4 and 25, three for 8.
  3. 3 and 9 divide 9, 99 and 999, so their tests add the digits; 11 divides 11, 99 and 1,001, so its test adds and subtracts them alternately.
  4. 6 and 12 are two tests each, 2 with 3 and 3 with 4, and the pairs are safe because neither has a common factor above 1; 7 shrinks the number instead.
