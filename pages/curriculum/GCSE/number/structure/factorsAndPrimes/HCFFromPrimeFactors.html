A common factor divides both numbers exactly

A common factor of two numbers is a factor of each of them. 1 is a factor of every whole number, so any two numbers have at least one common factor. The largest of them is the highest common factor, written HCF. The same number is also called the greatest common divisor, GCD: a divisor of a number is a factor of it, so the two names describe one thing.

Listing every factor of each number finds the HCF. The lists are short enough to write out while the numbers are small. 24 has eight factors and 36 has nine; the two lists share 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, and 12 is the largest.

HCF of 24 and 36 = 12

The HCF is the largest number that is a factor of both

  1. 12 is a factor of both: 24 = 2 × 12 and 36 = 3 × 12.
  2. No larger number is a factor of both: 18 divides 36 but not 24, and 24 divides 24 but not 36.

The HCF is everything the two numbers share

360 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 and 756 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 7. Each prime the two products share divides both numbers. Taking one shared 2 out of each leaves 180 and 378, which are still both even; taking out the second 2, then a 3, then the other 3, leaves 10 and 21, which share no prime. The four primes taken out multiply to 36, the HCF.

Taking the shared primes out of 360 and 756

Both numbers as rings of prime factors

360 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 and 756 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 7, with one sector for each prime factor.

Compare the prime factorisations

360 and 756 each have 24 factors. The two lists run to 48 numbers before the common ones can be compared. Written in index form, 360 = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5 and 756 = 2^2 × 3^3 × 7, and the two factorisations can be compared one prime factor at a time.

The HCF of 360 and 756

Write both numbers in index form

360 = 2³ × 3² × 5 and 756 = 2² × 3³ × 7, with equal primes in the same column.

  1. Factorise both numbers into index form, with prime bases in ascending order.
  2. Compare the two indices of each prime, counting a missing prime as index 0, and take the lower.
  3. Multiply the chosen prime powers. A prime whose lower index is 0 contributes no factor.

Take each shared prime to its lower index

A factor of 360 is built from the primes of 360. It contains no more factors of each prime than 360 does: 2^2 × 5 = 20 is a factor of 360, but 2^4 is not, because 360 contains only three factors of 2.

A common factor of 360 and 756 has to fit inside both numbers. For each prime, it contains no more factors of that prime than the number with fewer. 360 has three factors of 2 and 756 has two, so a common factor has at most two: the lower index. The largest common factor has exactly that many, and the same holds for every prime. The higher index fails the test: 2^3 × 3^3 = 216 is not a factor of 756.

An index counts the factors of a prime. 3^2 is two factors of 3, so 7^0 is no factor of 7, and writing each missing prime with an index of 0 lets the comparison cover every prime.

360 = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5^1 × 7^0
756 = 2^2 × 3^3 × 5^0 × 7^1
HCF = 2^2 × 3^2 × 5^0 × 7^0

The lower index of both 5 and 7 is 0, so neither prime is in the HCF. A power with an index of 0 is 1, which is why writing 7^0 leaves 360 unchanged; the reason is on the zero index.

HCF = the product of each prime to its lower index

  1. A prime missing from either number has index 0 there, so its lower index is 0 and it contributes no factor.
  2. A shared prime with the same index in both keeps that index: 108 and 540 both contain 2^2, so their HCF does too.

No shared prime

125 = 5^3 and 144 = 2^4 × 3^2 have no prime in common. Their only common factor is 1, so HCF = 1.

One number is a factor of the other

108 = 2^2 × 3^3 and 540 = 2^2 × 3^3 × 5. Every prime power in 108 is also in 540, so HCF = 108: the smaller number itself.

Every common factor is a factor of the HCF

The common factors of 360 and 756 are exactly the factors of 36. The HCF is not only the largest common factor: every other common factor divides it, because each is built from the same primes 2 and 3 with indices no higher than 2.

Divide both numbers by the HCF to check

360 ÷ 36 = 10 = 2 × 5
756 ÷ 36 = 21 = 3 × 7

Dividing by the HCF leaves quotients that share no prime, so their only common factor is 1. A prime common to both quotients shows that the HCF found is too small: multiplied by that prime, it still divides both numbers.

Find the HCF of two chosen numbers

600 and 252 share the primes 2 and 3; 125 and 144 share none; 108 and 540 share every prime of 108. In each case the HCF takes every prime to the lower of its two indices, and a prime with index 0 on either side is left out.

HCF of 600 and 252 = 2² × 3 = 12

Take the lower index, for every prime

  • Taking the higher index Choosing 2^3 × 3^3 for 360 and 756 gives 216, which does not divide 756: 756 = 3 × 216 + 108. The higher index belongs to the lowest common multiple, not the HCF.
  • Keeping a prime that only one number has Including the 5 and the 7 gives 2^2 × 3^2 × 5 × 7 = 1,260. That is larger than both 360 and 756, so it cannot be a factor of either.
  • Stopping at the first common factor found 6 divides both 24 and 36, but so does 12, the largest number in both lists. The HCF is 12, twice the 6 that was written down.

Summary

  1. A common factor divides both numbers exactly; the HCF is the largest common factor.
  2. For small numbers, list the factors of each and pick the largest that appears in both lists.
  3. For larger numbers, write both in index form; the HCF is every shared prime, each to its lower index.
  4. Leave out any prime that appears in only one of the numbers; with no shared prime, the HCF is 1.
  5. Divide both numbers by the HCF: the quotients share no prime.
