Drills

Converting between ordinary numbers and standard form in both directions, and multiplying, dividing, adding and raising numbers that are already written in it.

The test

Twelve questions and twenty-one marks, graded from routine conversion to questions that need a decision before any arithmetic starts. No calculator is needed: the numbers are chosen so that every answer comes out exactly. The rule itself, three worked examples and the mistakes that lose marks are on the teaching page, Standard form.

Marks are awarded as an exam would award them. A standard form answer carries two marks, one for the digits and one for the power, so a sound method with a slipped power still scores. There is no time limit. The clock starts when you write your first answer and is there only so you can see whether you are getting faster, which is what separates knowing a method from being fluent in it. New paper generates a fresh set of numbers testing the same twelve skills.
